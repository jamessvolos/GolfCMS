# SG Trainer — a simulated year of the caddie's note

Milestone 6 asked for a year of the explanation feature's roadmap, simulated. Each quarter below was written against the system as it actually ships — the rules in `lib/explain/rules.ts`, the engine's real fields, the 26 puzzles across 10 holes — and each was required to build on the prior quarter's evidence, name something that failed, and cut something as well as ship something. It is a thinking document, not a record: none of the numbers are measurements.

---

## Q1 — Instrumentation and Truth (first quarter after the caddie's note shipped)

We shipped a system whose entire claim is that every sentence is entailed by a number the engine computed — and then had no way to know which sentences we had said, to whom, or whether any of them changed a decision. Q1 was not about writing new rules. It was about building the receipt: making each note record itself, defining the smallest honest measure of "this taught something" (does the aim move toward the named line on the next encounter of a similar situation), and then reading what came back. What came back was that the notes are mostly true, occasionally not, and far more repetitive than we believed — and that the deeper problem is not the explanation layer at all. It is that a puzzle's difficulty is stored as one number computed at one profile bucket, so a 28-handicap is served a curriculum ordered by a 15-handicap's mistakes. We fixed three truth defects, killed a rule, wired a governor that had been dead since launch, and deliberately did not build more content.

### Shipped

**Cookie-scoped profileId (the precondition for everything else)** — v1 ships a single Profile row with id "local" (prisma/schema.prisma). A ledger keyed to one profile measures nothing. We added an opaque cookie-scoped profile id — no email, no password, no auth stack, no PII — so an invited cohort could play on one hosted instance and each player get their own Profile row, handicap, Elo, and streak.

*Why:* The spec puts auth explicitly out of scope for v1 and we did not want it back in. An opaque id is the smallest thing that makes a player a stable unit of observation.

*Cost:* It is not identity. Clearing cookies or switching devices mints a new player. We detect ghosts as profiles with fewer than 3 attempts and a handicap/speed/shape triple identical to an existing profile; ~9% of created profiles are ghosts and are excluded from cohort math. Every number in this quarter's evidence carries that 9% asterisk.

**The note ledger** — A NoteEvent row per rendered note: attemptId, rulesVersion, the ordered ruleIds (read slots then move), band, sgLoss, the value of the single key token, the correction's signed lat/long offset in yards, nSamples, whether the grid came from HeatmapCache or the worker fallback, and noteMs measured inside the worker. ~120 bytes.

*Why:* The note is generated client-side in a worker (lib/worker/engine.worker.ts) and never touches the server. The receipt has to be emitted by the thing that generated it. We store rule ids and the key numeral, not the rendered sentence — the sentence is a pure function of (ruleId, rulesVersion, engine inputs, DEFAULT_SEED) and is reconstructible offline by scripts/explain-golden.ts.

*Cost:* A second write per puzzle. The note is generated after the attempt POST fires and lands with the stamp beat (components/puzzle/PuzzleScreen.tsx around line 500), so it cannot ride the existing request. ~2% of attempts have no matching NoteEvent — the attempt committed, the note POST didn't. We report on the joined set and state the orphan rate rather than imputing.

**The transfer probe** — A situation class is the triple (category, dominant penal feature kind, the shot-frame side the MOVE named). On a player's next encounter of a puzzle in the same class, we take the signed lateral component of their aim relative to the previous note's named direction and record movedToward as a boolean. That is the whole measure.

*Why:* This is the smallest thing that can honestly be called learning. It does not require the player to score better, remember a number, or answer a question. It asks only whether the sentence moved the pin.

*Cost:* It needs two encounters in the same class, which the 26-puzzle library makes scarce — 612 qualifying pairs out of 3,612 attempts. And the classes are coarse: 'water, correction pointed right' lumps Sawgrass 18's approach in with Doral 18's tee. We accepted the coarseness rather than invent finer classes we couldn't populate.

**Multi-profile golden corpus as a build gate** — scripts/explain-golden.ts already ran the note over all 26 puzzles at one profile. We extended it to three buckets (h5/s110/straight, h15/s110/draw, h30/s90/fade) × two aim modes (at the flag, at the optimal) = 156 notes, committed as a fixture. Any rule's firing frequency moving more than 3 percentage points fails the build, and a diff of the rendered text is printed.

*Why:* Every defect in this quarter's failure list was found by running this, not by a user complaint. A rule set of 13 with interacting guards, tag diversity, and a band budget does not have a readable local behaviour; it only has a corpus behaviour.

*Cost:* ~9 minutes of CI. Grid computation is the expensive part (median 721ms, p95 1.84s per puzzle at h15), and we compute 78 grids. We cache them in CI by (puzzleId, bucket) the same way HeatmapCache does in production.

**Three truth fixes** — (1) fallback-strokes no longer prints a fabricated loss at zero loss. (2) club-down now guards on the printed value, not the raw one. (3) green-rate now respects floors.print instead of firing on greenShare > 0. All three are described in the failures section.

*Why:* The system's stated contract is that a claim which cannot verify itself does not ship. Each of these was a claim that verified itself against the wrong quantity.

*Cost:* fallback-strokes is the totality backstop — its guard is the literal constant true. Making it correct at zero loss meant it now has two sentences, which is the first branch in a rule that existed to prove every input produces a note. We added a corpus assertion that the backstop's share of notes never exceeds 10% at any bucket.

**The novelty governor, wired** — history now travels on WorkerRequest.note and is sourced from the player's last 8 NoteEvents. The demotion code in lib/explain/index.ts (seen in 3 of last 8 → stake × 0.35; in the last note → stake × 0.6) had never executed against a non-empty array in production.

*Why:* It was written, tested, documented in the selection comment as step 3 of 5, and dead. See failures.

*Cost:* Eight rows read per note generation, on the server, folded into the /play route's existing profile query. Zero cost on the reveal path — history is attached before the worker is asked for the note.

### The evidence

The corpus is the primary instrument; the ledger is the confirmation.

RULE CENSUS, aiming at the flag (the naive instinct), 26 puzzles per bucket. h5: green-rate 12, hold-the-line 11, club-clamp 9, penal-share 7. h15: penal-share 12, club-clamp 9, green-rate 7, cheap-bail 6, sub-resolution 6. h30: penal-share 17, club-clamp 11, cheap-bail 10, sub-resolution 10, green-rate 5. Read that h30 column again: a 28-handicap gets "That line puts N% of your Xs in the water" on 65% of notes and "Aim N yards left/right of that" on 38%. The ledger agreed within 1.5 points across 3,612 real notes. The corpus predicts production almost exactly, which was itself the first alarm — a novelty governor that was working should have pulled production away from the corpus.

BAND MIX BY BUCKET, same aims: h5 perfect 13 / good 4 / okay 3 / miss 6. h15 perfect 6 / good 7 / okay 7 / miss 6. h30 perfect 4 / good 5 / okay 10 / miss 7. Field sgLoss medians: handicap ≤ 8 → 0.04, 8–18 → 0.09, over 18 → 0.16.

AIMING AT THE OPTIMAL, all 26 land Perfect by construction, and the note collapses: hold-the-line 22/26 (85%), green-rate 11, fallback-strokes 6, room 2. A player who gets good sees one sentence, "Hold that line. It has N yards of room to the left," four times in five. The band budget gives Perfect the shortest note by design; the consequence nobody had looked at is that the shortest note is also the least varied one.

THE INCONVENIENT NUMBER — the transfer probe. Across 612 same-class re-encounters, 58% of aims moved toward the direction the previous note named, against a 50% null. That headline is almost entirely one rule. cheap-bail: 67% (n=214). free-gain: 61% (n=96). aim-line: 51% (n=143). club-down: 49% (n=71) — below chance, and club-down is the rule that names a club rather than a direction, so "moved toward" may simply be the wrong measure for it. It decays: first re-encounter 63%, second 57%, third 53%. And the worst of it: in the over-18 handicap group, movedToward is 54% and median sgLoss on the re-encounter does not improve (0.16 → 0.15, inside the noise of a 600-sample Monte Carlo). They move the pin where we told them and lose the same number of strokes. At handicap 30 the lateral sigma is 8.1% of shot distance — about 16 yards at a 200-yard approach — and the 0.10 corridor on most of our approach puzzles is narrower than that. We are giving instructions at a resolution finer than the player's own dispersion.

THE 40TH NOTE. The library is 26 puzzles. Median session is 11. 22 of the 61 active players exhausted it and entered lib/server/queue.ts review-by-recency mode, where pickNext returns repeat: true and re-serves the least-recently-played puzzle. Of 288 review re-serves, 96 (33%) produced the identical ruleIds in the identical order as that player's previous attempt on that puzzle, and 41 (14%) produced byte-identical sentences — DEFAULT_SEED is fixed, the grid comes from HeatmapCache, the bucketed profile is unchanged, so if the aim lands within about a yard the note is a literal replay. Mean distinct rules in a player's last 10 notes: 4.1 of 13. The answer to "what happens on the 40th note" is that on the 40th note the product starts quoting itself.

THE CROSS-HANDICAP FINDING, which turned out to be the biggest thing we learned. Puzzle.rating is seeded from trapSize = E[naive] − E[optimal] (constants.ts: 1000 + 1500 × clamp(trapSize/0.5, 0, 1)) and stored as one scalar, computed once at the seed profile. We recomputed trapSize per bucket across all 26 puzzles. Mean |Δrating| between h15 and h30 is 150 Elo — exactly the width of the queue's ±150 band. Nine of 26 puzzles move more than a full band. Spearman rank correlation of difficulty ordering, h15 vs h30: 0.46. (h15 vs h5 is 0.81, mean |Δ| 106 — the low end is fine.) Two examples carry it: harbourtown-18-tee-1 is 1524 at h15 and 1010 at h30 (the Lighthouse bunker is simply out of reach at 88 mph, so the tee shot has no decision in it); cape-01-approach is 1112 at h15 and 1659 at h30. pebble-8-approach-2 is 1000 at h15 — literally trapSize 0.000, a puzzle with no trap — and 1407 at h30.

And the sharpest single case: sawgrass-17-approach-1, the island green from the tee. At h5 the optimal line leaves 4% in the water. At h15, 11%. At h30, 27% — and trapSize at h30 is 0.005, meaning for a 28-handicap every aim on that hole is equally bad. The puzzle is rated 1144 in the database, so a 1200-rated 28-handicap gets served it, plays it, and the note truthfully reports: "Even the best line here leaves 27% of a pattern in trouble. Yours left 30%." Every word entailed. Nothing taught. The explanation layer is doing its job perfectly on a puzzle that should never have been in that player's queue.

PERFORMANCE. Note generation runs 13 probes (LADDER_LAT 8 + LADDER_LONG 5) plus one evaluateAim, 600 samples each = 8,400 Monte Carlo samples. Bench in Node: median 10ms, p95 16ms at h15, p95 44ms at h30. Field, from the ledger's noteMs: p50 34ms, p95 118ms, p99 260ms on the worst device in the cohort. The stamp beat runs 650–900ms, so the note has ~250ms of window and the p99 lands about 10ms late — one frame. The reveal budget was never the note's problem. It is the grid's problem (median 721ms, p95 1.84s), which is precisely why HeatmapCache exists, and 96.4% of production notes were generated against a cached grid rather than the worker fallback.

### What broke

- **The novelty governor has never run in production. Not once, from launch through week 5. lib/explain/index.ts implements it as step 3 of a documented five-step selection — a rule seen in 3 of the last 8 notes has its stake multiplied by 0.35, one seen in the last note by 0.6 — and the comment says this is 'how the maxims get their turn.' But WorkerRequest.note in lib/worker/protocol.ts has no history field, and lib/worker/engine.worker.ts passes history: [] as a literal. Attempt had no rule-id column, so history was not merely unsent — it was unreconstructable.**
  - Caught by: The ledger. Week 5, first full rule census: production rule frequencies matched the golden corpus (which runs with history: []) within 1.5 points on every rule at every bucket. A live governor should have pushed penal-share down and lie-widens/room up in repeat play. It hadn't moved anything. Three lines of grep confirmed it in about four minutes.
  - Response: Wired history through the protocol from the last 8 NoteEvents, and added a corpus test that runs a synthetic 12-note history and asserts the demoted rule's stake actually drops. The cost is what we lost: the first four weeks of ledger data cannot answer 'does variety help,' because there was no variety to vary. Worse, 1,880 attempts recorded before the ledger existed have no ruleIds at all and are permanently opaque — we know those players' aims and bands but not one sentence they read. That is the price of shipping a feature before its receipt.
- **fallback-strokes fabricated a loss. On a Perfect at sgLoss 0.00 the backstop printed 'That line plays 0.03 strokes worse than the best one on this hole.' The claim is strokes(Math.max(facts.floors.strokes, facts.sgLoss)) — the max was there to stop the note printing below the instrument's resolution, which is right, but at zero loss it converts a floor into an assertion. Six of 26 puzzles at the optimal aim, all tee shots: bayhill-18-tee-1, cape-01-tee, doral-18-tee-1, pebble-18-tee-1, pebble-8-tee-1, standrews-17-tee-1. It fires there because a perfect tee shot starves every other READ rule at once — club-clamp needs the aim clamped, penal-share needs a share the optimal doesn't also have, green-rate needs the green reachable, and 200 yards out none of them are true.**
  - Caught by: The multi-profile corpus. At h5 the Perfect count rose to 13 of 26 and the backstop's share rose with it; reading the h5 optimal-aim column made the sentence impossible to miss. Nobody reported it — you have to be good to see it.
  - Response: fallback-strokes now branches: below the strokes floor it says the line is inside the best one's resolution and prints no figure at all. Cost: the rule that existed as proof of totality — guard: () => true — now has a conditional, which is one more thing to keep honest, and it was live and wrong for the entire pre-fix quarter. The people it lied to were the ones who had just played the hole perfectly. That is the worst possible audience for a false number.
- **club-down printed 'Take a 7 iron — 140 yards instead of 140 yards.' The guard requires |optimal.aimDistance − playerAimDistance| ≥ 6, checked against raw yards; the sentence renders both through yardsMeasured, which rounds anything over 100 to the nearest 10. A 137/143 pair passes the guard and collapses in print. Reproduces on sawgrass-17-approach-1 at the h30 bucket.**
  - Caught by: The three-profile sweep, first run. Not visible at h5 or h15 on any puzzle in the library — the collision needs a shot distance over 100 yards with a 6-to-9 yard gap, and the shorter clubs of an 88-mph swing put more of the library into that window.
  - Response: club-down's guard now compares the formatted strings, and stands down when they are equal — the print resolution is part of the claim, not a display detail applied afterward. Cost: it cost us the assumption that the fact table and the formatter could be reasoned about separately. format.ts documents the choose-exact / measure-rounded split carefully, and this is the case where a guard on the exact value and a sentence on the rounded value disagree. We audited all 13 rules for the same pattern; club-down was the only one, but only because most rules print one number rather than two.
- **The ledger's own first version over-counted notes by 18%. components/puzzle/PuzzleScreen.tsx calls engine.note() unconditionally on every reveal, but only the first aim on a puzzle is a recorded attempt — re-aims are practice (attemptRecordedRef) and post nothing. Keying NoteEvent to attempts made 672 practice notes either orphaned or, in the first build, attributed to the previous attempt. It inflated penal-share's apparent frequency, because a player who drowns one re-aims and drowns again.**
  - Caught by: noteCount exceeded attemptCount in week 2. An arithmetic impossibility is the cheapest alarm there is, and it is the reason the ledger writes both counters.
  - Response: NoteEvent now carries a practice boolean and its own id; the transfer probe and every frequency number in this quarter's evidence use recorded notes only. Cost: week 1–2 numbers were re-derived and the first internal read of the census was wrong in the direction that flattered us — it made the notes look like they were engaging with hazards more than they were.

### Decisions and roads not taken

- **Make the note its own receipt. Emit ruleIds, rulesVersion, the key numeral, and the correction offset from the worker; store nothing else.**
  - The note is generated client-side in a worker with no server round trip, so whatever measures it has to be emitted by it. Storing ids rather than prose keeps the payload at ~120 bytes and keeps the ledger a pointer into a deterministic function: given (ruleId, rulesVersion, puzzleId, bucket, aim, DEFAULT_SEED), scripts/explain-golden.ts reproduces the exact sentence.
  - Rejected: A third-party analytics SDK — rejected because v1 has no accounts and one local profile, so a tracker buys us a session graph we have no identity to hang it on, and a consent surface we cannot honor. Storing the rendered sentence text — rejected as redundant with the deterministic reconstruction; the trade is that a copy change makes old rows unreadable, which we handle by stamping rulesVersion on every row and refusing to pool across versions.
- **Define teaching as aim movement toward the named line on the next same-class encounter, not as score improvement.**
  - It is the only thing in this system that is both observable and causally close to the sentence. The note names a direction; either the pin goes that way next time or it does not.
  - Rejected: sgLoss delta on re-encounter — rejected because it is confounded by construction: lib/server/queue.ts serves the nearest unseen puzzle within ±150 of a rating that rises as the player improves, so difficulty and skill move together and the delta measures neither. A thumbs-up/down on the note — rejected because self-report on a training tool measures novelty and tone, not learning, and it would have shipped as a number nobody could act on. A retention quiz — rejected as a different product.
- **Refuse a runtime LLM paraphrase pass to solve the repetition problem.**
  - Two hard constraints, both violated. The reveal budget is 900ms total and the note rides the stamp beat, which is 650–900ms — about 250ms of headroom, into which no network round trip fits, and the note already spends p95 118ms of it generating. And more fundamentally: every claim must restate itself from the facts via verify(). A paraphrase has no verify(). Rewriting 'That line puts 25% of your 7 irons in the water' into fresher prose produces a sentence whose truth is asserted by a model rather than entailed by outcomeStats, which is the one thing this feature exists not to do. We did not trade anything for it; we refused it and attacked repetition with the governor and with content selection instead.
  - Rejected: An offline LLM pass generating paraphrase variants at build time, sampled at runtime — rejected too, more reluctantly. It respects the budget and the variants could be human-reviewed once. But it multiplies the golden corpus by the variant count, and the moment a template has three phrasings the verify() functions that assert claim.text.includes(the computed percentage) become string-matching against a set. We may revisit it as a hand-written variant table, which is the same idea without the generator.
- **Keep the profile bucket at 5 handicap strokes and 10 mph. Instrument the drift rather than narrow it.**
  - HeatmapCache is keyed on (puzzleId, profileBucket) and a grid is ~900 cells × 600 samples. Halving the bucket roughly multiplies cold-cache computes by 4 across the library and moves the p95 grid time (1.84s) onto more players' first view of a puzzle.
  - Rejected: Narrowing to 2 strokes — rejected on cache cost. What we did instead is log the drift: a declared handicap of 28 is evaluated at 30, which is about 0.4 percentage points of extra lateral sigma, roughly 2 yards at a 200-yard shot. It is smaller than the effects we are chasing, and now it is a number in the ledger rather than an assumption. Also rejected: extending LATERAL_SIGMA_BY_HANDICAP past its last anchor at 20 with real data — we have 38 players over 18 handicap and no launch monitor, so we would be fitting a dispersion curve to aim intentions, not to shots.
- **Cut the room rule outright rather than loosen its guard.**
  - room requires the narrow side ≥ 6 yards, the wide/narrow ratio ≥ 1.8, and neither side clipped. It fired 0 times in 26 flag-aimed notes and 2 times in 26 optimal-aimed notes — 2 of 52. The fact it carries is corridor asymmetry, which hold-the-line already carries in the MOVE slot with a better sentence, because it tells the player which way to miss.
  - Rejected: Loosening the ratio to 1.4 to make it fire — rejected because then room (READ) and hold-the-line (MOVE) would compete for the same corridor number in the same note, and the tag-diversity check does not catch it: room is tagged 'room' and hold-the-line is tagged 'move', so the diversity rule would let both through and the note would state the corridor twice in four lines. Loosening the clipped check — rejected because a clipped walk means the corridor number is a floor, not a measurement, and a rule whose whole content is a measured width cannot honestly print a floor.
- **Do not re-rate puzzles per profile bucket this quarter. Log the drift and decide in Q2.**
  - The finding is real and large — mean |Δrating| of 150 Elo between h15 and h30, Spearman 0.46 — but the fix is a curriculum change, not an explanation change, and Q1's mandate was to learn what is happening.
  - Rejected: Shipping a PuzzleRating(puzzleId, profileBucket) table now — rejected for two reasons. First, it requires re-deriving trapSize per bucket, and trapSize is the difference between the naive line and the optimal, which at h30 is 0.005 strokes on the island green: we would be encoding 'this puzzle is easy' when the truth is 'this puzzle has no decision in it,' and those need different treatments (rating vs. exclusion). Second, the h30 grids depend on a lateral sigma that is linear extrapolation past the table's last anchor at handicap 20 — we would be baking an extrapolation into a curriculum. Also rejected: a 'same hole, two handicaps' comparison screen, which was the fun version of this finding. The evidence says the interesting fact is not that the optimal lines differ; it is that for a 28-handicap the optimal line is itself bad. A comparison view would dramatize a problem that content selection should be quietly solving.
- **Refuse a cross-puzzle memory rule ('you keep bailing right of everything').**
  - It was the most requested thing internally after the census showed how repetitive the notes are, and it is the one that most obviously breaks the architecture. Every rule's contract is that verify() restates the claim from the Facts table for this shot. A claim about the player's last eight shots cannot be restated from Facts, because the history is not in Facts and putting it there means the fact table stops being a snapshot of one evaluation and becomes a player model.
  - Rejected: Piping history into Facts so such a rule could exist — rejected. Note that this is a strictly different thing from the novelty governor, which only reorders claims that are each independently verifiable, and which is why the governor was safe to wire and this is not. If we want a player-model claim it belongs on the /summary page, computed server-side against the ledger, where it can be as slow and as auditable as it likes — not inside a 250ms worker budget.

### The numbers

Cohort and volume: 84 invited profiles created, 9% excluded as cookie ghosts, 61 with ≥10 recorded attempts. 3,612 recorded attempts joined to notes; 672 additional practice re-aim notes excluded after the week-2 fix; 1,880 pre-ledger attempts permanently unattributed. NoteEvent orphan rate 2.0%. Handicap spread: 9 under 8, 34 between 8 and 18, 38 over 18.

Truth (the primary metric, and the only one that can fail the build): sentences printed that the engine's own numbers do not entail — 6 of 26 puzzles at the optimal aim (fallback-strokes fabricating 0.03 at zero loss), 1 of 78 corpus notes rendering a degenerate comparison (club-down's "140 instead of 140"), 1 rule firing below its own print floor (green-rate at 4%). All three now zero, asserted in CI across 156 corpus notes.

Coverage and totality: every one of 26 puzzles produces at least one READ claim at all three buckets; a MOVE is offered on every note where a grid is present; band budget holds (Perfect ≤ 1 READ). Backstop share now capped at ≤10% per bucket, currently 0% at h15 and h30 and 3.8% at h5.

Repetition: mean distinct rules in a player's last 10 notes, 4.1 of 13. Top rule share by bucket at the flag — h5 green-rate 46%, h15 penal-share 46%, h30 penal-share 65%. At the optimal aim, hold-the-line 85%. Of 288 review re-serves after library exhaustion, 33% repeated the previous attempt's rule sequence exactly and 14% were byte-identical sentences.

Teaching (transfer probe): 58% of 612 same-class re-encounters moved toward the named line, against a 50% null. By rule: cheap-bail 67% (n=214), free-gain 61% (n=96), aim-line 51% (n=143), club-down 49% (n=71). By encounter index: 63% / 57% / 53%. By handicap: ≤8 → 62%, 8–18 → 60%, over 18 → 54% with no accompanying sgLoss improvement.

Curriculum integrity (the finding we did not go looking for): mean |Δ puzzle rating| between the h15 bucket the ratings were computed at and the h30 bucket, 150 Elo — the full width of the queue band. 9 of 26 puzzles drift more than one band. Spearman rank correlation of difficulty ordering h15↔h30, 0.46; h15↔h5, 0.81. Largest drifts: harbourtown-18-tee-1 −514, cape-01-approach +547, pebble-8-approach-2 +407.

Budget: note generation p50 34ms, p95 118ms, p99 260ms in the field, against ~250ms of stamp-beat headroom — the p99 arrives one frame late. 8,400 Monte Carlo samples per note (13 ladder probes + 1 player evaluation × 600). 96.4% of production notes generated against a cached grid; the 3.6% worker-fallback path pays the grid's median 721ms / p95 1.84s and is the only place the 900ms budget is genuinely at risk.

Rules: 14 → 13 (room deleted). rulesVersion incremented twice.

### Cut

Three things.

THE room RULE — deleted from lib/explain/rules.ts, 14 rules to 13. It fired 2 times in 52 corpus notes and 31 times in 3,612 production notes (0.9%). Its guard demands a narrow side ≥ 6 yards, a wide/narrow ratio ≥ 1.8, and both walks unclipped, which is a conjunction the real corridor geometry almost never satisfies. The fact it carried — corridor asymmetry from GridSummary.brief — is carried better by hold-the-line, which states the same number and also tells the player which way to miss. We rejected loosening its guard: at ratio 1.4 it fires often enough to matter and then collides with hold-the-line inside the same note, because tag diversity does not catch them (tags 'room' vs 'move') and the note would print the corridor width twice.

THE CONTENT WAVE. Q1's original plan was 10 more holes, roughly doubling the library to ~50 puzzles, and it was the single most expensive line item. We cancelled it in week 5 on the rating-drift evidence. Twenty-six puzzles rated at one profile bucket already give a 28-handicap a difficulty ordering with rank correlation 0.46 to their actual difficulty; fifty puzzles rated the same way gives them a longer wrong-ordered queue. Content is the right answer to the exhaustion problem and the wrong answer to the curriculum problem, and we have to fix the second before the first is worth paying for. Cost of the cut: the 26-puzzle library is exhausted in about 2.4 median sessions and 22 players have already hit review-by-recency, where a third of re-serves replay the previous note's rule sequence. We are knowingly shipping a repetition problem into Q2 to avoid multiplying a correctness problem.

THE NOTE RATING WIDGET. A thumbs up/down on each caddie's note, proposed as the fast way to know whether the notes land. Killed before it was built. Self-report on a training tool measures tone and novelty — a note is rated well the first time it says something surprising and rated poorly the fortieth time it says something true — and it would have shipped as a number that looked like signal, moved for the wrong reasons, and quietly become the metric we optimised. The transfer probe is worse-tempered, harder to populate, and honest. We also killed the related idea of surfacing an aggregate note quality score on /summary: the summary page is the player's record of their own play, and putting our self-assessment on it makes the product the subject.

### Still open

1. Is the transfer probe measuring instruction or geometry? cheap-bail is the only rule with a strong signal, and it is also the rule whose correction offsets are largest (the ladder's 12–24 yard rungs) and whose situations are most visually obvious — a pond. A player might move away from water without reading anything. The control we have not built: withhold the MOVE on a random 10% of notes and see whether the aim still moves. That is a live experiment on a teaching product and we have not decided whether we are willing to run it.

2. What is the honest instruction resolution for a 28-handicap? Their lateral sigma is ~8.1% of shot distance, which at 200 yards is wider than the 0.10 corridor on most of our approach puzzles. The system's own sub-resolution rule already refuses to correct inside the player's pattern for small offsets — but that guard uses max(6, sigmaLat) at the aim, and it may need to be the dominant behaviour at high handicap rather than an edge case. If the honest note to a 28-handicap on Sawgrass 17 is "there is no line here, take the bail-out," we do not currently have a rule that can say it, because we have no bail-out geometry in the data model.

3. Should some puzzles be excluded from some handicaps rather than re-rated? trapSize 0.005 at h30 on the island green is not "easy," it is "no decision." Rating and exclusion are different mechanisms and we have conflated them into one scalar since milestone 5.

4. Can the repetition problem be solved by content at all? 26 puzzles at a median 11 per session is 2.4 sessions to exhaustion. More holes is the obvious answer and we cut it this quarter on the grounds that a bigger library rated at one profile makes the curriculum problem bigger, not smaller. If Q2 fixes per-bucket difficulty, does the content argument change? Probably. But hand-tracing is the expensive part — the AI tracers produced a 13.6-acre fairway, a bunker over a swimming pool, and a hazard the classifier silently swallowed, and the one hole we benched for un-surveyable geometry is still benched.

5. Does the novelty governor actually help now that it runs? We have zero data on it — the entire pre-fix quarter is a control group with no treatment. The next census is the first real test, and if demoting penal-share simply promotes a less material claim, the governor is trading truth-weight for variety and should be reconsidered rather than tuned.

6. Is "the note taught something" even the right frame for the Perfect band? 85% of optimal-aim notes are hold-the-line. There may be nothing to teach a player who is already right, and the correct product move may be to say less rather than to say it differently — which is what the band budget already believes, and which the corpus suggests we have not followed far enough.

## Q2, year one — Coverage and the Long Tail

Q1 asked whether the notes were true and got a mostly-yes with three defects. Q2 asked the harder version — which situations does the rule set handle badly — and the answer was not the one the roadmap expected. The tail was not missing rules. `lib/explain/rules.ts` carried 16 entries after Q1 deleted `room`, and only 13 had ever printed a sentence to a player; `hazard-swap`, `penal-aggregate` and `leave` had passed their guards hundreds of times and never once reached a note, because they lost a materiality sort to constants and a tag contest to `penal-share`. Half the hazards we paid to hand-trace — 36 of 72 authored polygons are bunkers — belonged to a rule that had never spoken. So we confronted the long tail by deleting rules, not adding them: three penal rules merged into one kind-agnostic hazard rule, `leave` cut, MOVE routed through the same selection READ has always used, and a numeral guard that forbids a note from printing the same percentage twice. Registry 16 → 13, and for the first time every entry in it can print. Then the curriculum half: we did not re-rate puzzles per bucket (a promise from Q1 we deliberately broke), we excluded them — 19 of 120 (puzzle, bucket) pairs have no decision in them at that bucket and are no longer served there, which finally gets `sawgrass-17-approach-1` out of a 28-handicap's queue. And we bought content the cheap way: 14 new puzzles cut from the 10 holes already traced, zero new surveys. The quarter's real finding is the one in the failures column. CI truth has been zero for two quarters running. Field truth was not, twice — an authored label leaking a parenthetical aside into a player-facing sentence, and an authored lie the geometry contradicts. Both escapes came through content, not through rules. Explanation quality is a content problem wearing a generation problem's clothes, and we now have the receipts to say so.

### Shipped

**The registry/census reconciliation, and the admissible-but-silent counter** — Q1's census counted rules that printed. We added the other column: for every corpus note, which guards returned true and which of those claims never reached the page. Extended `scripts/explain-golden.ts` to emit, per note, `admissible[]` alongside `ruleIds[]`, and added a matching `droppedRuleIds` field to NoteEvent (the drop log) so a verify() failure in production is distinguishable from a guard that was simply false — in Q1 those two were the same row.

*Why:* The census was blind in exactly the direction the quarter was about. A rule that fires 0% looks identical to a rule that is never admissible, and the difference is the whole diagnosis: one is a content gap, the other is a selection bug. The instant we had the column we found three rules with a 27%/12%/4% admissibility and a 0% print rate.

*Cost:* NoteEvent grew from ~120 bytes to ~150. The drop log also told us something we would rather not know: 1.1% of production notes were carrying a claim that failed its own verify() and was silently swapped out, and we had shipped for two quarters without being able to see it.

**The dominant-hazard merge: penal-share + hazard-swap + penal-aggregate → one rule** — One READ rule now selects the single feature (or same-kind aggregate) with the largest `deltaShare × MARGINAL_COST` across water, OB, bunker and recovery, instead of three rules racing under one tag with water hardcoded first. The floors move with `nSamples` (hazard-swap's old literals 0.15/0.08 did not), the aggregate branch handles the multi-bunker case, and verify() restates the printed percentage against the same driver selection the claim used.

*Why:* 36 of 72 authored polygons in `data/holes/` are bunkers. `penal-share` matched only water and OB; `hazard-swap` matched bunkers, shared the tag `penal`, and MARGINAL_COST.bunker is 0.45 against water's 1.0, so any admissible water claim outranked it and tag diversity then blocked the second slot. Sand was structurally unsayable. It is now the printed hazard on 4 of 26 puzzles at h30 and 7 at h15 — 'That line puts 22% of your drivers in the sand short right' on `scottsdale-17-tee`, 'in the lighthouse bunker' on `harbourtown-18-tee-1`.

*Cost:* Three simple guards became one guard with a driver scan and a branch, which is more surface to keep honest — the same trade Q1 made on fallback-strokes and disliked then. It also failed the build twice (see failures). And the 3-percentage-point frequency gate had to be re-baselined across all 240 corpus notes, which means for one commit the gate protected nothing; we required two people to read the full rendered diff rather than the summary.

**MOVE goes through selection, in tiers** — MOVE was chosen by `for (const cand of scored.filter(s => s.rule.slot === 'move'))` — first passing guard in registry order. It never consulted stake and never saw the novelty governor Q1 spent a quarter wiring. Ordering is now the same machinery READ uses, with one addition the system needed anyway: every stake declares a tier. MEASURED means the number is derived from engine output (cheap-bail's `player.e − correction.e`, the merged hazard's delta-share); FLOOR means it is a priority constant (`hold-the-line` 0.01, `sub-resolution` 0.01, `lie-widens` 0.05). Measured always outranks floor; novelty may only reorder within a tier. `club-clamp`'s literal `stake: () => 1.0` became the measured gap, so it stopped automatically outranking every hazard claim on every clamped tee shot.

*Why:* This is why `hold-the-line` was 85% of optimal-aim notes in Q1 and why wiring the governor did nothing to it: registry order puts `hold-the-line` above `sub-resolution` and `aim-line`, forever, regardless of history. The documented five-step selection was running on half the note.

*Cost:* The tiering exposed that six of thirteen rules had never had a stake in strokes at all — they had priorities wearing strokes' clothes, in a field whose type comment says 'Strokes this claim accounts for'. Naming that honestly is better, but it is an admission that 'materiality' was partly fiction since launch. And it produced the quarter's worst result before it produced its best (see evidence).

**The numeral guard — one number, one claim** — A note may not print a percentage within `floors.print` of one already in it. Enforced in `explain()` at emit time, across READ and MOVE, before verification.

*Why:* `forced-carry` (tag 'forced') and the hazard rule (tag 'penal') were both admissible on the island green at h30 and tag diversity let both through: 'Even the best line here leaves 27% of a pattern in trouble. Yours left 30%.' followed by 'That line puts 30% of your 8 irons in the water.' Thirty percent, twice, four lines apart. This is the same defect class that killed `room` in Q1 — two rules with different tags competing for one number — and it recurred, which means tags were the wrong mechanism. 11 of Q1's 156 corpus notes printed a repeated numeral and nobody had looked.

*Cost:* The guard compares printed strings, so two genuinely different features that happen to both round to 12% collide and the second claim is suppressed. That happened on 4 of 240 corpus notes and we chose to accept it: a reader who sees 12% twice about two different things cannot tell them apart, so suppressing the second is the honest outcome even when the collision is a coincidence.

**Per-bucket exclusion — not per-bucket rating** — An offline job (`npm run content:fit`) recomputes trapSize for every (puzzle, profileBucket) pair from the same grids `HeatmapCache` would compute, and writes `data/fit.json` — 120 rows, committed and diffed in review. A pair with trapSize below `SCORE_BANDS[0].maxSgLoss` (0.03, the Perfect band) is marked no-decision and `pickNext` drops it from that bucket's candidate list. `Puzzle.rating` is untouched: still one scalar, still seeded at the reference bucket.

*Why:* Q1 measured 150 Elo of mean drift between h15 and h30 — the full width of the queue band — and named the trap: trapSize 0.005 at h30 on `sawgrass-17-approach-1` does not mean easy, it means every aim is equally bad. Rating and exclusion are different mechanisms and had been one scalar since milestone 5. Excluding is a sign test (is there a decision here at all), which survives the fact that lateral sigma past handicap 20 is a linear extrapolation; re-rating would bake that extrapolation into an ordering.

*Cost:* 19 of 120 pairs excluded — 3 at h5, 5 at h15, 11 at h30 — which shrinks the library most for the group already worst served. Before the situation wave a 28-handicap would have gone from 26 playable puzzles to 17. `pebble-18-layup-2`, our only par-5 layup, is excluded at h15 with trapSize 0.014: it is a layup puzzle, not a layup decision. And the exclusion is a queue filter, not a content gate, which cost us a hole we did not see until the ledger showed it (see failures).

**The situation wave: 14 new puzzles, zero new holes** — 26 → 40 puzzles, all cut from the 10 holes already traced and verified: 5 rough lies (the library had none — 12 fairway, 10 tee, 2 sand, 2 recovery), 3 more sand lies, 4 short-side approaches inside 45 yards, 2 second-shot layup decisions. Median authoring time 22 minutes per puzzle against roughly six hours per hole trace.

*Why:* Q1 cut the 10-hole wave on the rating-drift evidence and knowingly shipped a repetition problem: 26 puzzles at a median 11 per session is 2.4 sessions to exhaustion, and 22 players were already in review-by-recency where a third of re-serves replayed the previous rule sequence. Once exclusion existed, more content stopped multiplying a correctness problem. A puzzle is a ball position, a lie and a pin on geometry already verified against imagery — it is the cheapest content axis we have and we had never used it.

*Cost:* A situation can only be as interesting as the hole under it. All 10 holes are finishing holes and par-3s over water; re-cutting them cannot produce a genuine par-5 layup, an elevated shot, or a hole shape we do not own. The par-5 gap survives the wave untouched and is carried into Q3. Five of the rough-lie placements were also wrong (see failures).

**Label discipline at ingest** — `ingestSchema` now rejects hazard names containing parentheses, ampersands, or any string that reduces to nothing after directional stripping; `labelFeature` preserves authored casing instead of lowercasing the whole name; and we re-authored the 26 named penal polygons — deleting 14 names outright rather than forcing them through.

*Why:* `isProperName()` in `labels.ts` silently discarded 14 of 26 authored hazard names ('lake', 'left fairway bunker', 'front-right', 'back pot' all reduce to a bare kind or to nothing), and of the 12 it kept, three printed in a form no editor approved — including one authoring aside that reached players for nine weeks. Deleting a name is the right call when the computed alternative is better: 'the sand short right', derived from the hit locus, is more accurate than 'short-right bunker' authored from memory of a hole.

*Cost:* About an hour of content work and a permanent constraint on the annotate studio. It also means the folio's promise that hazards carry their real names is now honoured on 12 polygons out of 61 — we made the naming honest by making it rarer.

**Perfect says less** — On a Perfect band, MOVE prints only when a MEASURED-tier claim is admissible. Otherwise the note is the READ alone.

*Why:* Q1's open question six: 85% of optimal-aim notes were `hold-the-line`, and there may be nothing to teach a player who is already right. The band budget already believed this and we had not followed it far enough. `sub-resolution`'s sentence — 'That is the line. Play it again from here.' — is true and carries no instruction, so measuring whether it taught anything is a category error.

*Cost:* 61% of Perfect notes are now a single sentence. Whether a shorter note reads as respect or as the product having less to say is exactly the question the thumbs-up widget would have answered badly, and we killed that widget in Q1. We have no measure for this and shipped it on judgement.

### The evidence

THE REGISTRY AUDIT (the finding that set the quarter). `lib/explain/rules.ts` held 16 entries after Q1's `room` deletion. Q1's census reported 13 — because it counted rules that printed, and three had never printed a sentence in 3,612 production notes or 156 corpus notes. With the admissibility column added, at the flag across 78 corpus notes: `hazard-swap` guard-true 21 times, printed 0. `penal-aggregate` guard-true 9 times, printed 0. `leave` guard-true 3 times, printed 0. Each has a different cause and none of them is the rule. `hazard-swap` shares tag `penal` with `penal-share` and carries MARGINAL_COST.bunker 0.45 against water's 1.0, so it lost every materiality sort it entered and tag diversity then closed the second READ slot. `penal-aggregate` lost the same contest. `leave`'s stake is capped at 0.08 by `Math.min(0.08, …)` and it is a tee-shot rule, so it competed with `club-clamp`'s literal `stake: () => 1.0` on every clamped drive and lost 3 times out of 3. Across the whole registry, 31% of guard-true claims never reached a page.

THE CONTENT CENSUS behind it. 72 authored polygons across 10 holes: 36 bunker, 11 fairway, 11 recovery, 10 water, 10 green, 4 OB. Half the hazards in the library belonged to the rule that had never spoken. Puzzle lies before the wave: 12 fairway, 10 tee, 2 sand, 2 recovery — zero rough, so a third of `lie-widens`' guard (`sand || rough || recovery`) was unreachable by construction, and the rule could fire on 4 of 26 puzzles. Categories: 11 approach, 9 tee, 4 recovery, 2 layup.

THE NAMES. 26 of 61 penal polygons carry an authored name. `isProperName()` keeps 12 and discards 14: 'lake' (twice), 'lake left', 'left fairway bunker', 'short-right bunker', 'right greenside bunker', 'long-right bunker', 'back bunker', 'front-right', 'left-near', 'left-far', 'desert right', 'centre-line', 'back pot' — each reduces, after directional stripping, to a bare kind or to nothing. Of the 12 kept, the function returns `the ${name.toLowerCase()}` on the original string, so the directional words the comment says are 'stripped and re-derived' are not stripped at all in the printed branch, and proper nouns are flattened: 'Calibogue Sound marsh' prints as 'the calibogue sound marsh', 'Live oaks & villas right' as 'the live oaks & villas right', 'Road bunker' as 'the road bunker'. And 'far bank (off hole)' — an authoring aside on `sawgrass-18`'s OB polygon — passes the two-word test and prints verbatim.

RULE CENSUS AFTER THE MERGE AND THE MOVE FIX, at the flag, the 26-puzzle comparable subset. h30 before: penal-share 17, club-clamp 11, cheap-bail 10, sub-resolution 10, green-rate 5. h30 after: hazard 15 (4 of them naming sand — the first bunker sentences the product has ever printed), club-clamp 11 admissible but printing on 6 now that its stake is measured, green-rate 5, forced-carry 3; MOVE spreads to cheap-bail 7, sub-resolution 8, aim-line 6, hold-the-line 3, club-down 2. Top-rule share at h30 fell from 65% to 58%. At the optimal aim, where Q1 found `hold-the-line` on 85% of notes, it is now 41%, with `sub-resolution` at 28% and 61% of Perfect notes carrying no MOVE at all. Mean distinct rules in a player's last 10 notes: 4.1 → 6.2. Byte-identical re-serves: 14% → 6%; identical rule sequence on review re-serves: 33% → 19%, across 1,410 re-serves.

THE INCONVENIENT NUMBER, and it is the same shape as Q1's. Routing MOVE through the novelty governor diversified the note and made the teaching worse. Mid-quarter, across 1,180 same-class re-encounters, movedToward was 57% against Q1's 58% — flat, inside noise on its own. But decomposed: `cheap-bail`, Q1's only strong rule at 67%, fell to 61% (n=402), and the drop lived entirely in the notes where `cheap-bail` was admissible and got demoted for repetition, with a maxim printing instead. On those 64 notes movedToward was 52% — chance. The governor had traded a 67% rule for a 52% one, exactly as Q1's open question five predicted it might, and it did so on the players who most needed the bail-out: 41 of those 64 notes went to over-18 handicaps. We shipped the tier fix in week 9 — novelty reorders only within a tier, so a measured claim in strokes is never demoted below a floor-stake maxim — and `cheap-bail` recovered to 66% (n=180 post-fix) with overall transfer at 59% (n=1,940 full quarter). Variety is not free and it is not neutral; it is paid for out of the note's most material claim unless something stops it.

THE ONE GOOD RESULT, reported with its confound. In the over-18 group, movedToward went 54% → 58%, and median sgLoss on the re-encounter went 0.16 → 0.13. We are not headlining it. Excluding no-decision pairs mechanically changes which puzzles that group plays, so the two medians are computed over different puzzle sets — this is not a like-for-like comparison, it is partly the definition of the intervention. The Monte Carlo standard error on a single sgLoss at 600 samples is around ±0.01–0.02, so 0.03 sits at the edge of what the instrument resolves. What we can say cleanly is the mechanical part: a 28-handicap is no longer served `sawgrass-17-approach-1`, where trapSize at h30 is 0.005 and every aim is equally bad, and the note that used to be printed there — every word entailed, nothing taught — is not printed anymore because the puzzle is not served.

THE QUASI-EXPERIMENT WE COULD RUN, and its verdict. Q1 left open whether the transfer probe measures instruction or geometry, with the only clean control being a randomized withhold of the MOVE. We found a natural one instead: on the 2.9% worker-fallback path the grid is null, so `correction`, `corridor` and `toOptimal` are all null and no MOVE rule can fire — a population of notes with a READ and no instruction, assigned by cache miss rather than by us. 377 such notes this quarter; 58 had a same-class re-encounter. movedToward on those: 55% (95% interval roughly 42–68%) against 59% for MOVE-bearing notes. That interval contains both the null and the treatment effect. It is underpowered, it is confounded (cache misses cluster on first encounters and slow devices), and it answers nothing. We are reporting it as a non-result rather than dressing it up, and we are not building the randomized version (see cuts).

BUDGET. The merged rule replaced three fact-table scans with one; the numeral guard is a string compare over at most three claims. Field noteMs from the ledger: p50 33ms, p95 121ms, p99 258ms, against roughly 250ms of stamp-beat headroom — the p99 still lands about a frame late and we chose not to fix it. Still 8,400 Monte Carlo samples per note (LADDER_LAT 8 + LADDER_LONG 5 + one evaluateAim, 600 each). Cached-grid share 97.1%; the 2.9% fallback path still pays the grid's median 721ms / p95 1.84s and remains the only place the 900ms reveal budget is genuinely at risk. `npm run content:fit` runs 4m 40s in CI for 120 pairs, offline, and produces a diff a human reads.

### What broke

- **Five of the wave's rough-lie puzzles declare a lie the geometry contradicts. `PuzzleData.lie` is authored and drives club caps and the LIE_SIGMA_MULTIPLIER; landing lies are classified from polygons. We placed rough-lie balls by eye on satellite imagery, and on 5 of them `classifyPoint` returns fairway — the ball sits inside the traced fairway polygon. So `lie-widens` printed 'From the rough your pattern runs 1.25× as wide as it does from grass' about ground the engine itself calls fairway, and the dispersion those notes were computed from was 25% too wide.**
  - Caught by: `npm run content:audit` already checks this — and reports it as a warning (`!`), not a failure: `puzzle 3 declared rough, classifies fairway`. The lines were in the output. We read them during the wave and shipped anyway, on the reasoning that satellite fairway edges are soft and the authored lie represents intent. That reasoning is wrong in a product whose entire claim is that every sentence is entailed by a number the engine computed: the engine computed fairway. It was caught properly three weeks later when the corpus diff showed `lie-widens` firing on puzzles whose declared and classified lies disagreed.
  - Response: Balls re-placed on all five; the audit promotes a declared/classified mismatch from warning to failure with a non-zero exit for any non-tee puzzle; and `lie-widens` now guards on the classified lie agreeing with the declared one, so an authoring error makes the rule stand down instead of making it lie. Cost: 212 field notes across 34 players carried a sentence entailed by our authoring rather than by the hole, and this is the second time this year a defect reached players because a check we already owned was advisory. Warnings that ship are not checks.
- **The merged hazard rule's verify() selected its driver by raw fraction while the claim selected by `deltaShare × MARGINAL_COST`. On any note where the largest-share feature is not the largest-stake feature — a wide fairway bunker catching 24% next to an OB strip catching 9% — verify() restated a different polygon than the claim named, failed the text match, and the claim was dropped in silence. With the hazard claim gone, `fallback-strokes` took the READ.**
  - Caught by: The build failed, on the backstop-share cap Q1 shipped: ≤10% per bucket, and h30 came back at 13.5% across the corpus. That cap existed because Q1 wanted totality to be provable, not because anyone expected it to catch a driver-selection bug a quarter later.
  - Response: verify() now recomputes the driver through the identical selection function the claim uses — one function, called twice, rather than two expressions that agree by inspection. Cost: three days, and a more uncomfortable realisation. A dropped claim and a guard that returned false are the same row in the census, so this class of bug is invisible by construction — it does not raise an error, it just makes the note quieter. That is what forced the drop log into the quarter's scope, and the drop log's first three weeks showed 1.1% of production notes carrying a verify failure, 78% of them this rule during its bad week. Steady state is now 0.3%, effectively all `club-down` standing down on its formatted-string guard, which is Q1's fix working as designed.
- **'That line puts 9% of your drivers in the far bank (off hole).' The OB polygon on `sawgrass-18` carries the authored name 'far bank (off hole)' — a note to the tracer about which side of the water the polygon belongs to. `isProperName` strips 'far', finds two remaining words, and returns the original string verbatim. The parenthetical went to players.**
  - Caught by: Found by hand while writing the label lint, not by any instrument. Then counted the way Q1 designed for: notes are not stored as text, they are reconstructed deterministically from ruleIds plus rulesVersion plus the cached grid, so we could replay every note that ever named that polygon — 187 of them, 9 weeks, 41 players, concentrated at h25 and h30 where dispersion is wide enough for the guard's anchor floor to clear on a tee shot.
  - Response: Names validated at ingest (no parentheses, no ampersands, nothing that reduces to a bare direction), casing preserved on print, and 14 authored names deleted in favour of the computed kind-plus-locus phrasing. Cost: the Q1 decision not to store rendered sentence text was justified here — it let us count the damage exactly — but it also means we had no way to notice the damage, because nobody reads 3,612 reconstructions. The lesson is narrower than 'store the text': the golden corpus is the only surface where a human ever reads what the product says, and it had 156 notes covering 26 puzzles at aims we chose. A phrase reaches players through combinations the corpus does not enumerate.
- **Exclusion is a queue filter, not a content gate. `pickNext` skips no-decision pairs; the home listing in `app/page.tsx` still lists all 40 puzzles, and `/puzzle/[id]` still serves any of them. So a 28-handicap could still click into `sawgrass-17-approach-1` from the library and get the note we had just decided should not exist for them.**
  - Caught by: The ledger, in the first week after ship: 63 recorded notes landed on excluded (puzzle, bucket) pairs, 91% of them entered from the home listing rather than /play. We had validated the change against `queue.test.ts` and never asked how else a puzzle gets on screen.
  - Response: We did not hide them. The library is the player's, not the queue's, and a player who wants to play the island green should be able to play the island green. The home listing now marks the pair rather than removing it. This sits uncomfortably close to the Q1 decision that the product's self-assessment does not belong on the player's own pages, and the discomfort is real — the difference we are claiming is that 'this puzzle has no decision at your profile' is a statement about the puzzle, computed from trapSize, not a statement about how well we think we explained it. Cost: two surfaces that must agree about the same `data/fit.json`, and if they drift the player gets a marked puzzle in their queue or an unmarked one they should not see.

### Decisions and roads not taken

- **Confront the long tail by merging three rules into one and deleting a fourth, taking the registry from 16 entries to 13 while making bunkers sayable for the first time.**
  - The tail rules were not underpowered, they were unreachable: `hazard-swap` was admissible on 27% of corpus notes and printed on none, because it shared tag `penal` with `penal-share` and carried a lower marginal cost. Adding a bunker rule would have added a fourth competitor to a contest whose loser was already predetermined. Selecting the dominant hazard across all penal kinds in one rule fixes the cause instead of the symptom, and every rule added multiplies the interaction surface — the numeral collision between `forced-carry` and the hazard rule is what that surface costs when it grows.
  - Rejected: A dedicated bunker rule — rejected because it inherits the same tag contest and the same marginal-cost ordering, so it would have printed as rarely as `hazard-swap` did. Splitting the tags so a water claim and a sand claim can both appear — rejected outright: two percentages about two hazards in a four-line note is precisely the defect that killed `room` in Q1, and we are not going to relearn it a third time. Loosening `hazard-swap`'s literal 0.15/0.08 thresholds so it fires more — rejected because the thresholds were never the binding constraint; it passed its guard 21 times and printed zero.
- **Give every stake a declared tier — MEASURED (derived from engine numbers, in strokes) or FLOOR (a priority constant) — and let novelty reorder only within a tier.**
  - The Claim type documents stake as 'strokes this claim accounts for', and six of thirteen rules returned a literal: `club-clamp` 1.0, `lie-widens` 0.05, `hold-the-line` 0.01, `sub-resolution` 0.01. A single sort key over two incommensurable things is a fiction, and it produced two visible harms: `club-clamp` outranked every hazard claim on every clamped tee shot regardless of the hazard's size, and the novelty governor demoted a 0.31-stroke bail-out below a 0.01 maxim. The rule we settled on states itself in one line: novelty may reorder claims that are within a band of each other; it may not outrank a tenth of a stroke.
  - Rejected: Tuning the 0.35 and 0.6 demotion multipliers — rejected because we would be fitting two constants to a single quarter's 6-point effect on one rule, which is how a governor becomes folklore. Turning the governor off after the transfer regression — rejected: repetition is real (4.1 distinct rules in 10 notes, a third of review re-serves replaying the previous sequence) and the governor is the only mechanism that touches it. Inventing real stroke stakes for the maxims — rejected because there is no honest one; the value of 'hold that line' is not measured in strokes by anything the engine computes, and inventing a number for a sort key is how you end up printing it.
- **Exclude (puzzle, bucket) pairs with no decision in them; do not re-rate puzzles per bucket.**
  - Q1 promised to decide this quarter and the decision is to do the smaller thing. Exclusion needs only a sign test — is trapSize at this bucket below the Perfect band, 0.03 — and that test survives the fact that LATERAL_SIGMA_BY_HANDICAP's last anchor is handicap 20 and everything past it is linear extrapolation. A rating is an ordering, and an ordering built on an extrapolation is a curriculum built on an extrapolation. It also keeps the two mechanisms distinct, which was the actual conceptual error since milestone 5: `harbourtown-18-tee-1` at h30 is not a 1010-rated easy puzzle, it is a tee shot with no decision in it because the Lighthouse bunker is out of reach at 88 mph.
  - Rejected: A `PuzzleRating(puzzleId, profileBucket)` table — rejected for the extrapolation reason above and because it encodes 'easy' where the truth is 'empty'. The 'same hole, two handicaps' comparison screen — rejected again, for the reason Q1 gave and one more: now that exclusion exists, the comparison view would dramatise a problem the queue is quietly solving. A bail-out rule that says 'there is no line here, take the medal route' — rejected because there is no bail-out geometry in the data model (`FeatureKind` has no such concept and inferring one from the fairway polygon would be a guess), and more fundamentally because a note that says 'there is nothing to say' is still a note we chose to print on a puzzle we chose to serve. The honest fix is not serving it.
- **Buy content as situations on traced holes, not as new holes. 14 new puzzles, 0 new surveys.**
  - A puzzle is a ball position, a lie and a pin over geometry already verified against imagery — 22 minutes against roughly six hours. It directly filled the two gaps the census named: the library had zero rough lies, so a third of `lie-widens`' guard was unreachable, and only 2 layup situations. And it offsets exclusion, which took 11 pairs away from h30 exactly where the library was thinnest.
  - Rejected: The 10-hole wave, cut for a second consecutive quarter — the tracing cost is unchanged, the AI tracers that produced a 13.6-acre fairway and a bunker over a swimming pool are unchanged, and `riviera-10` is still benched in `data/holes-draft/` for the reason written there: Esri resolves ~0.25 m/px at that green and no edge-detection method we tried found the putting surface boundary. Sizing a green from published dimensions is not a trace. The OSM importer — rejected as out of v1 scope and, more usefully, because the defect class we most fear is 'a hazard the classifier silently swallowed', and an importer industrialises exactly that. We accept the consequence: a situation is only as interesting as the hole under it, all ten of ours are finishing holes and par-3s over water, and the wave cannot manufacture a par-5 layup decision. That gap is now Q3's problem and it costs a trace.
- **Refuse the hand-written paraphrase variant table, closing the door Q1 left ajar.**
  - Q1 rejected a runtime LLM outright (it cannot exist inside a 250ms worker budget and cannot be entailed by construction) and rejected an offline generator more reluctantly, noting a hand-written variant table is 'the same idea without the generator'. Having now measured where repetition actually comes from, we are refusing it. The merge and the MOVE fix moved distinct rules per 10 notes from 4.1 to 6.2 and byte-identical re-serves from 14% to 6% — that is variety of content. A variant table buys variety of wording, multiplies the golden corpus by the variant count, and turns every `verify()` that asserts `claim.text.includes(computedPercentage)` into set membership against phrasings. The corpus is the only place a human reads what this product says; making it three times larger makes it read less.
  - Rejected: Varying DEFAULT_SEED per attempt so the Monte Carlo numbers differ on a replayed puzzle — rejected and worth naming explicitly, because it is the cheapest-looking fix in the codebase. It would make the golden corpus non-deterministic and, worse, it would mean a player who aims at the same point twice reads 29% and then 31%. The percentage is the load-bearing token; jittering it to relieve boredom trades the only thing the product has. We accept the residual: aim within about a yard of a previous attempt on the same puzzle at the same bucket and you will read the same sentence, and that is correct behaviour.
- **Do not shrink the probe ladder to bring the p99 inside the stamp beat.**
  - Field p99 is 258ms against roughly 250ms of headroom — about one frame late, on the worst device in the cohort, on 1% of notes. Dropping LADDER_LAT to four rungs would cut roughly a third of the 8,400 samples and land it, but the 6-yard rungs are what makes `chooseCorrection`'s contract honest: it returns the smallest offset that reaches Good, and with 12-yard rungs the smallest offset available becomes 12 whether or not 6 would have done. 'Aim 12 yards left' when 6 would do teaches over-correction, and a rule that names a distance the player can execute is the one rule that measurably transfers (66%). The trade: one frame, on 1% of notes, on the slowest phones, to keep the correction resolution at half a grid cell.
  - Rejected: Precomputing the ladder into HeatmapCache alongside the grid — rejected because the ladder is measured from the player's own aim, which is not known until they drop the pin; caching it would mean caching probes around the optimal, which is the exact thing `probes.ts` exists not to do. Deferring the note past the stamp beat — rejected because the note rides the stamp beat by design and a late note reads as a second event.
- **Kill the randomized withhold-the-MOVE experiment permanently, and report the natural experiment as a non-result.**
  - Q1 left it open. The design is sound — withhold the MOVE on a random 10% of notes and see whether the aim still moves — and we are still not going to run it, because the population it would run on is the over-18 group whose transfer is weakest and whose sgLoss does not improve, and deliberately withholding the instruction from the players it helps least is not a thing we are willing to do to learn something about ourselves. The fallback-path substitute gave 55% (n=58) against 59%, an interval that contains everything, confounded by cache misses clustering on first encounters and slow devices. We are stating plainly that we cannot separate instruction from geometry with the instruments we are willing to use.
  - Rejected: Withholding only from low-handicap players, where the effect is strongest and the stakes lowest — rejected because it answers a different question (does the note teach a 5-handicap) than the one we care about (does it teach a 28-handicap). Waiting for the fallback population to grow — rejected: it is 2.9% and falling as cache coverage improves, so the natural experiment gets weaker every quarter by design.

### Cut

Four things, and the largest of them is the one we cut for a second consecutive quarter.

THE `leave` RULE — deleted from `lib/explain/rules.ts`. It was guard-true on 3 of 78 corpus notes and printed on none, losing every contest to `club-clamp`'s literal 1.0 stake, and the tier work would have rescued it. We deleted it instead, for a reason that has nothing to do with frequency: its sentence — 'Both lines are in play; the better one leaves 42 yards less club into the green' — is a claim about the next shot, in an engine that plays one shot. Carry equals total, there is no roll-out, and multi-shot play-out is explicitly out of v1 scope. The value of being 42 yards closer is already priced inside `expectedStrokes` through the baseline table; saying it out loud as a separate benefit implies a play-out the engine never performed. It was the only rule in the registry whose subject was a shot the product does not model. Cost: the layup situations the wave just added are exactly where `leave` would have spoken, and they now lean on `depth-error` and `aim-line`, which say less.

THE SECOND CONTENT WAVE, AGAIN. The 10-hole trace was cut in Q1 on rating-drift evidence and cut again this quarter on cost. Once exclusion existed the Q1 objection dissolved — a bigger library rated at one profile is no longer a longer wrong-ordered queue, because the queue now drops the pairs with no decision in them — and we still did not buy it. Fourteen puzzles from existing geometry cost about five hours in total; ten holes cost roughly sixty, plus the review burden that caught a 13.6-acre fairway, a bunker over a swimming pool, and a hazard the classifier silently swallowed. `riviera-10` stays in `data/holes-draft/` where the README says why: Esri resolves about 0.25 m/px at that green and no method we tried found the putting-surface edge, so the current green is a published dimension placed inside a bunker ring, which is a guess wearing a trace's clothes. What this cut costs is specific and we are carrying it: the library still has no genuine par-5 layup decision, no elevated shot, no hole that is not a finishing hole or a par-3 over water. Situations are cheap and holes are not, and we have now spent both of the cheap quarters.

THE RANDOMIZED WITHHOLD EXPERIMENT — killed permanently, not deferred. Q1 listed it as the only clean control on whether the transfer probe measures instruction or geometry, and named the discomfort without resolving it. The resolution: the population where the answer matters is the over-18 group, whose movedToward is weakest and whose sgLoss barely improves, and suppressing the instruction for a random tenth of their notes to learn something about our own attribution is a cost we are placing on the wrong people. We ran the natural version instead — the 2.9% worker-fallback path produces MOVE-less notes assigned by cache miss — got 55% against 59% on 58 re-encounters, an interval containing both hypotheses, and are reporting it as a non-result rather than a hint. We are stating for the record that `cheap-bail`'s 63% may be substantially players moving away from a visible pond, and that we do not intend to find out.

THE PER-BUCKET RATING TABLE — cut as a design, not merely deferred. Q1 postponed `PuzzleRating(puzzleId, profileBucket)` to this quarter; this quarter decided not to build it at all. Ratings are an ordering and orderings inherit every error in their inputs, including a lateral sigma that is linear extrapolation past handicap 20. Exclusion asks one question with a sign — is there a decision here — and that question survives an input the ordering would not. `Puzzle.rating` stays one scalar computed at one bucket, with 150 Elo of known drift and a 0.46 rank correlation between h15 and h30, and we are living with it on purpose. If Q3 shows players being served badly *inside* their playable set rather than at its edges, this comes back.

### Still open

1. What is a note for, on a puzzle with no decision in it? We solved the sawgrass-17-at-h30 case by not serving the puzzle, which is right for the queue and does nothing for the library — a player who clicks in from home still gets a truthful note that teaches nothing, now with a label on the card. The rule we refused to write ('there is no line here, take the bail-out') is still the honest sentence, and we still have no bail-out geometry to entail it with. Adding one is a `FeatureKind` change, a re-annotation of ten holes, and a new class of authored claim. Worth it only if no-decision situations turn out to be common rather than the 16% of pairs we found.

2. `club-down` has now sat at chance for two quarters (49%, then 50%, n=163). Either the rule does not transfer, or movedToward — a signed lateral component against a named direction — is simply the wrong instrument for a claim whose content is a club number. The obvious fix is a second measure (did the player's aim distance move toward the named carry) and the obvious risk is that inventing a second measure per rule is how you end up with a metric that always says yes. Decide in Q3 whether to build it or to stop reporting `club-down` in the transfer table at all.

3. Is the tier rule stable, or did we just move the argument? Novelty now reorders only within a tier, which fixed the `cheap-bail` regression. But it also means the three MOVE maxims can only ever print when nothing measured is admissible, so their share is now a function of how often the engine finds a correction — which is a property of the content, not of the teaching. If the Q3 content is more penal, the maxims vanish again and the note diversity we bought this quarter quietly unwinds.

4. Half the authored hazard names are gone by our own hand, and the computed alternative ('the sand short right', from the hit locus) is more accurate but less like a caddie. The folio's whole claim is that hazards have names. We have no evidence either way about whether a named hazard lands differently than a positioned one — and the only cheap way to get that evidence is self-report, which we killed in Q1 for reasons that still hold.

5. The par-5 layup gap survived the situation wave and cannot be closed by it. Neither can elevation, wind, or any hole shape we do not own. Q3 either pays for traces or admits the library's ceiling is the ten finishing holes we have. `riviera-10` remains the test case: it is still benched for un-surveyable geometry, and nothing about this quarter changed the imagery.

6. Exclusion is computed from trapSize at a bucket, and the h30 bucket's grids rest on a lateral sigma extrapolated past LATERAL_SIGMA_BY_HANDICAP's last anchor at handicap 20. We argued that a sign test survives an extrapolation the way an ordering does not, and that argument is sound for pairs far from the 0.03 threshold. It is not sound for the pairs near it. We do not know how many of the 19 are near it, and we should.

7. Two truth escapes this quarter, both from content, both through a check we already owned and had made advisory. The pattern is not 'we need more checks'. It is that content review and code review are the same activity in this product and only one of them has a gate.

## Q3, year one — Transfer, and What a Note Cannot Do

Q1 asked whether the notes were true. Q2 asked which situations they handled badly. Q3 asked the question the product's whole thesis rests on and had never tested: does any of this change what a player does when they are standing on a tee three weeks later, on a hole we have never traced? We built two instruments to find out and both came back unflattering. The first was a decomposition of the transfer probe we should have run in Q1: split same-class re-encounters by whether the previous encounter was on the same hole. Within a hole, movedToward is 64%. Across holes, it is 53%, and in the over-18 group it is 50% — the null. For three quarters we have been reporting a 58% headline that is substantially players remembering a hole. The second was dwellMs, the time from the note landing to the player's next action: median 4.8s in a player's first ten notes, 1.5s after their sixtieth, and 47% of notes past attempt 40 get under 1.2 seconds, which is not reading. So the quarter's finding is that the caddie's note — the thing we have spent a year making true, varied and material — is a per-shot object being asked to teach a per-player habit, and past the fortieth note it is barely being read at all. What we shipped follows from that: the instruction moved into the reveal's geometry where a 1.2-second look can still catch it (the one clean win: movedToward on sub-1.2s notes went 54% → 59% with dwell unchanged); a player-level surface off the 250ms budget, with its own registry, guards and verify(), computed server-side against the ledger; a pocket card that leaves the product; and a diary check-in that is honestly labelled as not evidence. We deleted green-rate, cut the LLM narrative that fits the new surface better than it ever fit the note, cut club-down from the transfer table rather than invent a measure that would flatter it, and — for the first time in three quarters — paid for two hole traces, not for variety but because our ten holes put their trouble on the left 17 times out of 26 and a player-level lateral claim is uninterpretable on a library that biased.

### Shipped

**dwellMs — the receipt for "is it read at all"** — NoteEvent gains dwellMs: milliseconds from the end of the stamp beat (the moment the note is on screen) to the player's next action — Next, a re-aim, or navigation away. Visibility-corrected: the Page Visibility API pauses the clock and any sample where the document blurred is dropped rather than clamped. ~4 bytes on a row that was already ~120.

*Why:* Q1 built the ledger to know which sentences we said. Q2 built the drop log to know which we silently didn't. Neither could answer whether anyone read them. Every teaching number this product reports — 58%, 59%, the by-rule table — implicitly assumes the note was consumed, and that assumption had never been checked.

*Cost:* It is a proxy and a Goodhart trap in waiting; see open questions. The first version was wrong in the direction that flattered us (see failures).

**The offset stake: the MOVE, drawn** — Note.mark has carried { at, delta, glyph: 'spot-height' } since launch and rendered as an unlabelled tick. It is now a surveyed offset: a leader line from the player's dropped pin to correction.aim, labelled with the same yardsExact string the MOVE sentence prints, drawn in the reveal's second beat with the dispersion ellipses rather than left to the text in the third. No new engine work — correction.aim and player.e − correction.e are already computed by chooseCorrection() before the note exists.

*Why:* Direct consequence of dwellMs. If half of experienced players give the note under 1.2 seconds, the answer is not to make the sentence better, it is to put the instruction where a 1.2-second look already lands: the map. The offset is the one part of the MOVE that survives being glanced at.

*Cost:* The map now states a number that the sentence also states, which is exactly the collision that killed the room rule in Q1 — we accepted it here because the two are the same claim in two modalities rather than two claims about the same corridor, and the numeral guard was extended to treat the mark's label as part of the note for duplicate-percentage purposes.

**The tendency card — a second registry, server-side** — lib/pattern/rules.ts: five rules with the same shape as lib/explain (guard, stake in strokes, claim, verify), computed in getProgress() against the ledger joined to Attempt, rendered on /summary. lateral-bias (median signed shot-frame offset of the player's aim from the bucket's optimal, expressed in units of their own sigmaLat), depth-bias, clamp-habit (how often they aim past their own max carry — club-clamp at the player level), hazard-cost (which FeatureKind takes the most strokes off them), category-cost. Every claim carries its n and its distinct-puzzle count in the sentence.

*Why:* A tendency is a distribution and a note is a measurement; no single note can honestly say 'you keep bailing right of everything', and Q1 explicitly refused to let one try. But that claim belongs somewhere, and /summary has no 250ms budget, no worker, and can be as slow and auditable as it likes — which is the argument Q1 made when it refused the cross-puzzle memory rule.

*Cost:* It speaks to 19 of 198 active profiles. Three gates (n ≥ 12 in class, ≥ 8 distinct puzzles, both-side hazard exposure) plus a two-sided sign test at p < 0.05 are what make it honest, and together they exclude 90% of the cohort. It also ended the quarter at four rules, not five (see cuts).

**The pocket card** — A single printable/saveable card generated from the player's strongest tendency claim: the claim itself with its n, the counter-instruction phrased in the MOVE's own vocabulary, and a three-item pre-shot checklist derived from it. Static HTML, no state, nothing to log in to, generated on demand from the same pattern rules.

*Why:* The only artefact this product has ever made that is meant to leave it. If transfer means anything, it means something the player carries to a first tee, and a web page they have to remember to open is the most we can build without identity.

*Cost:* 41 of 198 generated one; 23 opened it more than once. That is the honest ceiling of a pull-only instrument, and we cut the push (see cuts).

**The round check-in** — Opt-in card on /summary: 'played a round since your last session?' → three structured items in the note's own vocabulary (did you face a forced carry / a bail-out decision / a club-down decision; what did you do; which side was the trouble). No free-text field. 61 check-ins from 26 profiles over nine weeks.

*Why:* It is the only channel this product has to the world outside it. Q1 killed the note rating widget because self-report on a training tool measures tone and novelty; this is a different object — self-report about a decision the player made on a golf course is data about the world, not about us.

*Cost:* n = 61, self-selected, unverifiable, and we report it labelled 'diary, not evidence' every time it appears. No free text, because free text is PII-shaped and this product holds none by design.

**Attempt.profileBucket (and NoteEvent.profileBucket)** — The bucket string the grid was computed at, stored on the row. Backfilled null; anything null or mismatched is excluded from the tendency card.

*Why:* Forced by the quarter's field truth escape. An aim is only comparable to an optimal computed at the same bucket, and Attempt had no idea which bucket it was played at.

*Cost:* Everything before week 6 is unusable for the card for the 14 players who crossed a bucket boundary. Third time this year a single scalar computed at one bucket has produced a wrong answer.

**Two hole traces, bought for hazard side — the first new geometry in three quarters** — firestone-16 (par 5, 625y, pond short-right of the green) and bethpage-4 (par 5, cross bunkers, trouble right off the tee): 7 new puzzles including the library's first two genuine layup decisions with a real decision in them at more than one bucket. A third trace, merion-11, was authored and benched (see failures/cuts). ~13 hours of tracing and review against Q1's estimate of six hours per hole.

*Why:* Not for variety and not for exhaustion. Across the 26 base puzzles the dominant penal polygon sits left of the ball→pin line on 17 and right on 9; all four recovery puzzles are left; the only near-balanced class is approach. A player-level lateral-bias claim on a library that lopsided measures the library. This is the first time the content argument has been a measurement argument.

*Cost:* Both are par 5s, so we closed Q2's layup gap and did nothing about elevation, wind, or any hole shape the engine cannot model. They shipped in week 10 — too late to move this quarter's eligibility numbers, so the effect on the card is stated as a projection, not a result.

**scripts/pattern-golden.ts — the second corpus** — Nineteen anonymized ledger fixtures (the eligible profiles, aim points and outcomes only, no ids) × the pattern registry, committed and diffed in CI. Any claim whose printed n, sigma multiple or sign does not restate from the fixture fails the build, exactly as explain-golden does.

*Why:* The tendency card is a new class of claim on a new surface. Q2's lesson was that both truth escapes came through surfaces the golden corpus does not enumerate; shipping a second registry without a second corpus would have been shipping the same mistake knowingly.

*Cost:* The fixtures are real players' play, frozen. When the cohort changes the fixtures do not, so the corpus tests the rules and not the population.

### The evidence

THE DECOMPOSITION WE SHOULD HAVE RUN IN Q1. The transfer probe defines a situation class as (category, dominant penal feature kind, the shot-frame side the MOVE named). It has never cared which hole the previous encounter was on. Split it: of 2,910 same-class re-encounters this quarter, 1,540 were on the same holeId as the note being tested against and 1,370 were on a different hole. Same-hole movedToward: 64%. Cross-hole: 53%, 95% interval 50.3–55.6. The pooled figure is 58%, statistically indistinguishable from Q1's 58% of 612 and Q2's 59% of 1,940 — the headline has not moved in a year, and it now looks like it never measured what we said it did. By rule, cross-hole against same-hole: cheap-bail 56% (n=214) against 71% (n=286); free-gain 55% against 63%; aim-line 50% against 55%. By handicap, cross-hole only: ≤8 → 57%, 8–18 → 54%, over 18 → 50% (n=520). The group the product exists for shows no cross-hole transfer at all. Q2 reported over-18 transfer rising 54% → 58% as its one good result, hedged for the exclusion confound; that rise was entirely within-hole.

DWELL. dwellMs, visibility-corrected, 21,400 recorded notes. Median overall 2.4s. By the player's own attempt index: 1–10 → 4.8s, 11–30 → 3.1s, 31–60 → 1.9s, 61+ → 1.5s. 47% of notes after a player's 40th attempt are under 1.2 seconds. The median note is 24 words across two sentences; at 250 wpm that is 5.8 seconds and at a hard skim of 400 wpm it is 3.6. Under 1.2 seconds nobody is reading anything but the band stamp and the key numeral. By band: miss 3.4s, okay 2.6s, good 2.0s, perfect 1.3s — the Perfect note is the least read of the four, which is the band budget's own belief confirmed from the other direction and an argument for having followed it further, not less. By READ rule: the merged hazard rule 2.9s, club-clamp 2.5s, forced-carry 2.2s, lie-widens 2.1s, fallback-strokes 1.4s, green-rate 1.3s. green-rate was the least-read READ in the registry and, at h5 in Q1, its most frequent one.

THE ONE CLEAN WIN, and how far we can push it. The offset stake shipped in week 4, alone, no other change in that build. Restricting to the 88 players active in both windows to control for cohort composition and attempt index: on notes where dwellMs was under 1.2s, movedToward went 54% (n=610) to 59% (n=688). On notes with dwell ≥ 2s it was 62% before and 62% after. The effect appears exactly where the mechanism predicts and nowhere else, and dwellMs itself did not move (2.5s → 2.4s), so it is not that the mark made people read. It is not randomized and we did not randomize it, for the reason Q2 gave when it killed the withhold experiment permanently. It is the strongest result this product has produced and it is still a pre/post.

DWELL AND TRANSFER, with the confound named first. Among notes with dwell ≥ 2s, movedToward is 62%; under 1.2s, 54%. This does not mean reading causes transfer. A player who has already decided to change their aim has a reason to read, and a player who glances is telling us they had nothing to reconsider. The direction is suggestive and the causality is unavailable to us without the experiment we refuse to run.

THE LIBRARY'S SIDE BIAS, measured directly from data/holes/. For each puzzle, take the largest water or OB polygon and project its centroid into the shot frame. Across the 26 base puzzles: 17 have their dominant penal feature left of the ball→pin line, 9 right. By class: approach 6 left / 5 right, tee 6 / 3, layup 1 / 1, recovery 4 / 0. After Q2's situation wave the 40-puzzle library runs 26 left / 14 right, with recovery at 6 / 0. The consequence for a player-level claim is fatal: 'your aim sits right of the best line' on a library whose trouble is left two-thirds of the time is a statement about our ten holes. This is why lateral-bias carries a both-side exposure gate (≥ 3 distinct puzzles with the dominant feature on each side) and why it is ineligible in the recovery class by construction, and it is why we bought traces. The two new holes add 5 right-dominant puzzles against 2 left; projected library 28 / 19, approach class 10 / 11.

THE TENDENCY CARD'S REACH. 198 profiles with ≥ 10 recorded attempts. 34 pass the n ≥ 12 in-class gate. 19 pass all gates and see a card. Rule firing among those 19: category-cost 15, lateral-bias 11, clamp-habit 9, hazard-cost 7, depth-bias 4. The card prints at most two claims, mirroring READ_BUDGET. clamp-habit is the one that surprised us: 'On 14 of your last 38 tee shots you aimed past your own driver carry' fires on players who have read the club-clamp sentence dozens of times, which is the first direct evidence we have that a per-shot claim repeated is not a habit changed.

CATEGORY VERSUS HAZARD DIAGNOSIS, and why we cannot tell you. We shipped both deliberately to compare them. At n = 19 card holders we can distinguish neither: subsequent three-week sgLoss in the category a card named moved 0.01, inside the Monte Carlo standard error of a single sgLoss at 600 samples. What we can say without any statistics is that category-cost is a prose restatement of the CategoryStat table that has been on /summary since milestone 5. It told nobody anything they could not already read off a table, and we cut it mid-quarter.

THE DIARY, labelled. 61 check-ins from 26 of 198 profiles. 38 named a bail-out decision, 9 a club-down decision, 6 hold-the-line, 8 none of the three. The interesting row is club-down: it is 8% of MOVEs printed in production but 15% of reported on-course decisions — the only signal we have ever had on the rule the transfer probe has scored at chance for two consecutive quarters (49%, then 50%). It is 9 self-reports. It is not evidence. It is enough to conclude that movedToward — a signed lateral component against a named direction — is the wrong instrument for a claim whose content is a club number, which is what Q2's open question two suspected, and it is the basis for removing club-down from the transfer table rather than building it a bespoke measure.

BUDGET. Deleting green-rate removes one guard and one stake evaluation from every note. Field noteMs: p50 31ms (was 33), p95 118ms, p99 251ms against ~250ms of stamp-beat headroom — the p99 still lands about a frame late and is still unfixed by decision, for the third quarter. Still 8,400 Monte Carlo samples per note (LADDER_LAT 8 + LADDER_LONG 5 + one evaluateAim × 600). Cached-grid share 97.6%; the 2.4% worker-fallback path still pays the grid's median 721ms / p95 1.84s and is still the only genuine risk to the 900ms reveal. The tendency card is a page cost, not a reveal cost: /summary p95 went 190ms → 640ms on first ship and back to 340ms after indexing NoteEvent(profileId, createdAt) and Attempt(profileId, puzzleId). Nothing about the pattern registry touches the worker, which was the whole point of putting it there.

### What broke

- **The tendency card told six players the opposite of the truth about their own aim. lateral-bias compares each stored Attempt.aimPoint to the optimal for that puzzle at the profile's bucket — but Attempt has no bucket column (schema.prisma: aimPoint, sgLoss, band, eloDelta, xpGained, ratingAfter, and nothing else), so the card read HeatmapCache at the profile's CURRENT bucket and compared old aims to a new hole's best line. 31 of 198 profiles changed handicap or club speed in /settings during the quarter; 14 crossed a BUCKET_HANDICAP_STEP (5) or BUCKET_SPEED_STEP (10) boundary; for 6 of them the sign of the claimed tendency flipped.**
  - Caught by: An internal tester who had corrected their handicap from 20 to 14 was told their aim sat a median 0.7σ left of the best line across 21 approaches, on a set of shots they specifically remembered pushing right. Nothing failed. The claim verified — it restated correctly from the numbers it was given, which were the wrong numbers.
  - Response: profileBucket added to Attempt and NoteEvent, backfilled null, and the card excludes any row whose bucket is null or differs from the profile's current one. Cost: for those 14 players everything before week 6 is gone from the card, and 3 of the 19 eligible profiles fell below the distinct-puzzle gate as a result — the fix cost us three of the nineteen people the surface exists for. The deeper cost is that this is the third time in one year the same mistake has produced a wrong answer: Puzzle.rating as one scalar at one bucket (Q1), exclusion computed at one bucket (Q2), and now an aim compared to an optimal from a bucket it was never played at. Any number compared across time has to carry the bucket it was computed at, and we have now learned that three times.
- **The dwell instrument over-counted, and the correction made the finding worse. The first version measured render → next action with no visibility handling. 8.4% of samples exceeded 60 seconds — background tabs, players who walked away — and the distribution was bimodal with a bump past 45s that the median quietly absorbed.**
  - Caught by: By plotting the distribution instead of reading the median. The median said 3.6s, which is a plausible skim of a 24-word note, and we very nearly wrote that down as 'the note is being read'. The histogram said otherwise.
  - Response: Page Visibility clamping, and samples where the document blurred are dropped rather than truncated. Median fell 3.6s → 2.4s and 'under 1.2s past attempt 40' went 34% → 47%. Cost: three weeks of a number that said the product was fine, quoted once in a planning doc before it was corrected. The instrument we built to check an assumption arrived carrying the same assumption, and the correction moved every headline in the direction that indicts us — which is the only reason we trust it.
- **The card counted attempts, not situations. One real card read 'Across 43 approaches your aim sits a median 0.6σ right of the best line; on 31 of 43 it was right.' Twenty-two of those 43 were the same four puzzles, replayed in queue.ts review-by-recency mode after library exhaustion. The sign test was computed over 43 correlated samples of four decisions.**
  - Caught by: Reading an actual card for an actual player rather than the aggregate — the same failure mode as Q2's label leak, and caught the same way, by a human looking at one rendered artefact.
  - Response: The gate now requires ≥ 8 distinct puzzleIds as well as ≥ 12 attempts, and the sentence prints both counts so a reader can see the difference. Cost: eligibility fell from 34 profiles to 19. We spent the quarter building a surface that reaches 10% of the cohort, and the gate that made it honest is what took it there. It also means the 26-puzzle exhaustion problem Q1 cut content to avoid now has a second consequence nobody predicted: a library small enough to replay is a library too small to draw a player model from.
- **verify() proves the number, not what the sentence means by it. An offline LLM pass over the 282-note corpus — used as an adversarial reader, never as an author — flagged green-rate's 'That pattern finds the green 34% of the time, averaging 25 feet from the flag' as reading like the whole pattern averages 25 feet, when facts.player.greenFeet is computed from onGreen samples only. The number is entailed. The sentence, as a competent reader parses it, is not.**
  - Caught by: The critic pass filed 11 flags; a human triaged them to 3 real ones. CI has been green on truth for three consecutive quarters and could never have caught this: verify() asserts claim.text.includes(the computed figure), which is a check on the numeral and says nothing about its referent.
  - Response: We deleted green-rate rather than reword it (see cuts), and the other two flags — depth-error's 'the number was 20 yards short' being ambiguous between the aim and the outcome, and 'the sand short right' parsing as a description of the player's shot — are logged and unfixed. Cost: the honest admission that the architecture's central guarantee is narrower than the claim we have been making for it all year. 'Every sentence is entailed by a number the engine computed' is true. 'Every sentence means what the reader thinks it means' is not checked by anything except a human reading the corpus, and the corpus enumerates aims, not readings.
- **merion-11 was traced correctly and is unsayable. Its defining hazard is a brook roughly 5 yards wide crossing the approach. GRID_SPACING_YDS is 6, so the lattice can miss it entirely between cells; at 600 Monte Carlo samples from 150 yards at h15 it catches 2.6% of the pattern, and floors.anchor is 0.08. The merged hazard rule can never fire on the hole's whole point.**
  - Caught by: Running explain-golden over the draft hole before ingest, as content review now requires. Every note on all three buckets came back describing the green and the bunkers and never once mentioned the water, which is the only thing a golfer standing there is thinking about.
  - Response: Benched in data/holes-draft/ alongside riviera-10, with the reason written in the README: the hole is correct and the instrument cannot resolve it. Cost: one of three traces we paid for, about four hours, and a new category of content rejection we did not have a name for. Q1 rejected a hole for un-surveyable geometry. This one is perfectly surveyable and sub-resolution — the same sentence the sub-resolution rule says to a 28-handicap, said back to us about our own content.

### Decisions and roads not taken

- **Decompose the transfer probe by hole and report the split permanently, retiring the pooled figure as a headline.**
  - The pooled 58% has been stable for three quarters and we now believe it was stable because it was measuring hole familiarity, which our 26-then-40-puzzle library guarantees an abundance of. Same-hole 64% and cross-hole 53% are two different claims and only the second is about course management. The split costs nothing to compute — NoteEvent already carries the attempt and the attempt carries the puzzle — and it should have existed in Q1.
  - Rejected: Keeping the pooled number as the headline with the split as a footnote — rejected because we would have kept quoting the flattering one, and we know that because we did it for two quarters. Redefining the situation class to include holeId, which would have made every re-encounter cross-hole by construction — rejected because it destroys the comparison: we would lose the ability to see the gap, which is the finding. Waiting another quarter for a larger cross-hole n — rejected; 1,370 is enough to exclude the effect being large.
- **Build the player-level claim as a second rule registry on the server, with its own guards, stakes, verify() and golden corpus — not as a rule inside explain(), and not as prose.**
  - Q1 refused a cross-puzzle memory rule and gave the right reason: history in Facts means a claim that cannot verify itself from the facts of a single shot, inside a 250ms worker budget. Nothing about that changed. What changed is that we finally built the place Q1 said such a claim belongs — server-side, against the ledger, as slow and auditable as it likes. The registry shape is copied deliberately: if the claim is not restatable from the fixture, it does not print, exactly as in lib/explain.
  - Rejected: Piping history into Facts so a note could say it — rejected again, unchanged. Computing the card on the client from the last N attempts — rejected because the honest version needs a sign test over the full history and a bucket join, which is a query, not a hook. Putting the tendency line inside the note as a third sentence — rejected hardest of all: a note is about this shot, the card is about this player, and mixing them means a sentence with a different evidentiary standard sitting in a paragraph the player has learned to trust at a glance.
- **Gate every pattern claim on distinct puzzles, both-side hazard exposure and a two-sided sign test, and accept that the card reaches 19 of 198 players.**
  - The library's own geometry forced this: 17 of 26 base puzzles have their trouble left, recovery is 4 for 4 left, and a lateral claim drawn from that measures our content. The alternative was a claim we could not defend when a player asked why. Nineteen honest cards is a product; 198 unfalsifiable ones is a horoscope.
  - Rejected: A provisional version with a hedge — 'you may tend to aim right' — rejected on a constraint the codebase already enforces: BANNED_LEXICON in format.ts contains 'tends to', 'might', 'could', and 'chance'. The system has no hedging adverbs by construction because the floors are the hedge. Adding them for a new surface would have unpicked the one discipline that has held for three quarters. Lowering the sign-test threshold to p < 0.10 — rejected because at n = 12 that is one flipped shot. Showing the card with the claim greyed out and an 'x more shots to unlock' counter — rejected because it turns an evidentiary gate into a progress bar, and the player would then play to unlock it.
- **Refuse an LLM-authored pattern narrative on /summary. Accept an LLM as an offline adversarial reader of the golden corpus, never as an author.**
  - This is the strongest case for a generator the product has ever had and it deserves the honest version of the argument. The pattern card sits on a server-rendered page with no 250ms budget, no determinism requirement in the reveal, and no worker. The rules cost us a registry, a corpus, three gates, and they speak to 19 of 198 people. A model reading the ledger would write something for all 198 tonight. The reason we still refused: the card's claims are sign tests, and a paraphrase of a sign test is not a sign test — it drops the n, the distinct-puzzle count and the eligibility, which are the entire reason the claim is allowed to exist. Worse is the provenance problem. If /summary is generated and the note is not, the player has no way to know which surface carries a guard, and the note's credibility is the whole product. Two truth escapes this year both came through content — surfaces the corpus does not enumerate — and a generator is an unbounded content surface by definition. The trade, stated plainly: we are choosing 19 players getting a card we can defend over 198 getting one we cannot.
  - Rejected: A runtime paraphrase pass on the note — refused in Q1, still refused, and now also on the 900ms budget and the determinism of the corpus. An offline hand-written variant table — refused in Q2, still refused. A model that writes the card and a rule that verifies it afterwards — rejected as the worst of both: the verifier would be string-matching a free paraphrase, which is precisely the failure mode Q1 named when it killed the variant table. What we did accept: a critic pass that reads the 282-note corpus and files flags for a human to triage. It found the green-rate referent defect that three quarters of green CI could not (see failures). It writes nothing that ships.
- **Move the instruction into the reveal's geometry rather than making the sentence work harder.**
  - dwellMs says the note past attempt 40 gets a glance. A glance lands on the map, the band stamp and the key numeral — all three of which we control. The offset stake uses numbers already computed by chooseCorrection(), costs no Monte Carlo, and rides beat two of a reveal that already draws contours and ellipses. It is the only change this quarter with a clean pre/post result.
  - Rejected: Typographic escalation — bigger, bolder, animated — rejected because it is fighting a 1.2-second read with ornament, and the folio's discipline is that the identity lives in the data language, not in emphasis. A dwell gate: a Next button that unlocks after three seconds — rejected on two grounds, that coercing attention on a training tool is contemptuous of the player, and that it would destroy dwellMs, which is the only instrument we have for the question. Shortening every note to one sentence — rejected as a bigger change than the evidence supports; the miss-band note at 3.4s median is being read, and it is the one that matters most.
- **Delete green-rate rather than reword it.**
  - It is a description, not a diagnosis: it names no decision, no direction and no alternative. Because it names no direction it cannot generate a situation class, so the transfer probe has never been able to score it — for three quarters it has been the h5 bucket's most frequent READ and completely invisible to the only teaching measure we have. It is the least-read rule in the registry at 1.3s median dwell. And its sentence is misreadable in a way verify() structurally cannot catch. Four independent reasons pointing the same way.
  - Rejected: Rewording it to 'of the shots that find the green, the average is 25 feet' — rejected because the fixed sentence is still undiagnostic and still unscoreable; we would have paid a rulesVersion bump to keep a rule whose only defence is that it sounds informative. Demoting it to an unselected footer stat under the note — rejected on the Q1 principle that a stat we choose to print is a sentence we chose to print, and putting it outside the selection path puts it outside verification too. Keeping it only at h5, where it was most frequent — rejected because 'this claim is fine for good players' is not an argument about the claim.
- **Stop reporting club-down in the transfer table. Do not build it a second measure.**
  - Two quarters at chance (49% n=71, 50% n=163). movedToward is a signed lateral component against a named direction and club-down names a club; the measure and the claim are about different axes. The diary's 9 mentions, at 15% of reported decisions against 8% of printed MOVEs, is the only evidence we have and it points at the instrument, not the rule. Reporting a number that we believe does not measure the thing is worse than reporting nothing.
  - Rejected: The obvious fix Q2 named — a second measure asking whether the player's aim distance moved toward the named carry — rejected because a bespoke measure per rule is how a metric learns to always say yes, and we would then have to defend why cheap-bail is scored laterally and club-down longitudinally when both could be scored both ways. Deleting the rule — rejected: it is materially staked, it fires on real clamped situations, and 'we cannot measure it' is not evidence that it is useless.
- **Buy two hole traces, chosen for hazard side and for a real layup decision — not for fame — and bench the third.**
  - Content has been cut for two consecutive quarters on the grounds that it answered the wrong problem. This is the first quarter the content argument has been a measurement argument: 17 of 26 base puzzles have their dominant penal feature left, recovery is 4 for 4 left, and no amount of statistical care makes a lateral-bias claim interpretable on that library. Two right-dominant par 5s also close Q2's par-5 layup gap, which the situation wave could not manufacture.
  - Rejected: The ten-hole wave, cut for a third consecutive quarter — at roughly six hours per trace plus the review burden that has already caught a 13.6-acre fairway, a bunker over a swimming pool and a hazard the classifier swallowed, and now a hole whose hazard is below the instrument's resolution. riviera-10, benched for a third quarter; the Esri imagery has not changed and a published green dimension placed inside a bunker ring is still a guess wearing a trace's clothes. Mirroring an existing hole to balance the sides — the cheapest possible fix and rejected outright: a reflected Sawgrass 17 would balance the library and be a lie, and the imagery is the product's evidence. The OSM importer — rejected again, on the same grounds and with a new one: merion-11 shows that a correct trace can still be unusable, and an importer produces correct traces at a rate no review can keep up with.
- **Refuse the three-weeks-later nudge, and therefore refuse identity, again.**
  - The single most effective thing we could do for transfer is reach a player the evening before their round. That needs an email address, which needs accounts, which Q1 refused by design — opaque cookie-scoped ids, no email, no password, no PII. We are not reversing a privacy decision to send a reminder. The pocket card is the pull-only version and 41 of 198 pulled it, which is the honest ceiling.
  - Rejected: An in-app 'welcome back' after a 14-day gap — rejected narrowly: it reaches someone already in the product, and the moment that matters is the first tee, not the home page. An ICS/calendar export so the player schedules their own nudge — rejected as a plausible-sounding feature nobody would use once. Optional email, opt-in only — the closest call of the quarter, and rejected because 'optional' PII is still PII in the database, still a consent surface we would have to honor, still a breach we could have, and the thing it buys is a reminder. If Q4 concludes the transfer question is unanswerable without identity, that is a decision to make on its own terms and not as a side effect of a nudge.

### Cut

Five things.

THE green-rate RULE — deleted from lib/explain/rules.ts, registry 13 entries to 12. It was the h5 bucket's most frequent READ at 46% in Q1 and it is the least-read sentence in the product at 1.3s median dwell. Four reasons, independent: it names no decision, so it is a description; it names no direction, so it can never generate a situation class and the transfer probe has been structurally blind to it for three quarters; its sentence is misreadable in a way verify() cannot catch, because verify() asserts the numeral and not its referent; and it is the cheapest thing in the note to skip. We rejected the reworded version because a fixed sentence is still undiagnostic and still unscoreable, and we rejected demoting it to a footer stat because a stat we choose to print is a sentence we chose to print, and moving it outside selection moves it outside verification. Cost, and it is real: backstop share at h5 rose from 2.5% to 7.9% against a 10% cap, h5's top-rule share re-concentrated on club-clamp at 44%, and approach notes at h5 got shorter in a way that reads thin. We also deleted the only sentence in the registry that ever said something good about a shot. Nothing else in the product tells a player that their pattern found the green.

THE category-cost PATTERN RULE — cut mid-quarter, pattern registry 5 to 4, after it fired for 15 of the 19 eligible players and told none of them anything. 'Your approaches are costing you 0.14 strokes against your own median' is a prose restatement of the CategoryStat table that has sat on /summary since milestone 5, with a worse presentation and a longer sentence. It was also the one pattern rule whose gate was loose enough to fire for almost everyone, which is precisely why it was worth suspecting. The lesson generalises past this rule: a player-level surface earns its place by saying something a table cannot, and the first thing we built was the table again.

THE THREE-WEEKS-LATER NUDGE — cut, along with the identity system underneath it, for the third consecutive quarter that this product has declined to know who its players are. Email or push the evening before a round is the single highest-leverage transfer intervention available and it requires an address, an account, a consent surface and a breach we could have. Q1 refused PII by design and nothing this quarter is worth reversing that for. We shipped the pull-only version instead — the pocket card — and 41 of 198 players generated one, 23 opened it twice. That number is the cost of the cut, stated plainly: if transfer requires a push, this product as currently constituted cannot do transfer, and we would rather carry that as a known limit than fake it with an in-app banner.

THE LLM-AUTHORED PATTERN NARRATIVE — cut, and this was the closest call of the year, because /summary is the one surface where every argument that killed the runtime paraphrase in Q1 and the variant table in Q2 does not apply. No 250ms budget. No worker. No determinism requirement in the reveal. Prose about a person over weeks, which is exactly where a rule set is weakest and a model is strongest, and the rules we shipped instead speak to 19 of 198 players. We refused it anyway on two grounds. First, the card's claims are sign tests, and a paraphrase of a sign test silently drops the n, the distinct-puzzle count and the eligibility gate that are the only reasons the claim was allowed to exist. Second, mixed provenance is worse than either pure choice: if the summary page is generated and the note is not, the player cannot tell which sentences carry a guard, and the note's credibility is the entire product. The trade is explicit and we are carrying it: 19 defensible cards over 198 we could not verify. What we did accept is an offline critic pass — a model that reads the golden corpus and files flags for a human, and writes nothing that ships. It found the green-rate referent defect that three quarters of green CI could not.

club-down IN THE TRANSFER TABLE — cut as a reported metric, not as a rule. Two quarters at chance (49%, then 50%) on a measure that resolves the signed lateral component of an aim against a named direction, applied to a claim whose content is a club number. Q2 named the obvious fix and the obvious risk in the same breath; we chose the risk-free half and stopped reporting it. The diary's 9 mentions — 15% of reported on-course decisions against 8% of printed MOVEs — is the only evidence we have that the rule works at all, it is nine self-reports, and it is enough to conclude that the instrument rather than the rule is what has been failing. Cost: one of five MOVE rules is now formally unmeasured, and we have set a precedent that a rule can stay in the registry with no teaching evidence behind it, on the strength of an argument about instruments. That precedent needs watching.

### Still open

1. Cross-hole transfer is 53%, and 50% in the over-18 group. Either the product teaches holes rather than principles, or the probe cannot see a principle when it transfers. The clean test is a hole the player has never seen, which is content, which we have cut in three of four quarters — and the two holes we did buy shipped in week 10 and were bought for a different reason. Q4's first job is to serve the new geometry to players who have exhausted the old and measure cross-hole transfer on genuinely novel ground. If it is still 53%, the thesis is in trouble and we should say so rather than instrument around it.

2. The tendency card reaches 19 of 198. Is a registry, a corpus, a schema migration and a quarter of engineering justified by a surface that speaks to a tenth of the cohort? The honest answer depends on whether the gates are permanent or transitional. The distinct-puzzle gate loosens as the library grows; the both-side gate loosens as the library balances; neither loosens quickly. If Q4's projection holds and eligibility reaches 40%, this was infrastructure. If it stays under 20%, it was a beautiful thing built for almost nobody.

3. The diary is not evidence and we keep saying so, which does not make it evidence. Either Q4 builds a real study — a small paid cohort, a scorecard, ideally a launch monitor so LATERAL_SIGMA_BY_HANDICAP stops being extrapolated past its anchor at 20 — or we stop making on-course claims in the product's own copy. The spec's vision paragraph says this trains the fastest scoring gains available to a mid-handicap player. Nothing we have measured in a year speaks to a score on a card.

4. dwellMs is a Goodhart trap with a fuse on it. It is already tempting to design for it, and the first design we would reach for — a slower reveal, a gate, a bigger stamp — is exactly the coercion we rejected this quarter. Decide in Q4 whether dwell stays a reported metric or becomes a diagnostic we look at when something else moves.

5. verify() proves the numeral and not the referent, and the only instrument that can catch a misreadable sentence is a human reading the corpus. We now have two corpora, 282 notes and 19 cards, and nobody reads either end to end more than once a quarter. The LLM critic pass found the one defect three quarters of green CI missed. Do we make that pass a build step — a model that reads and flags but never writes — and if so, how do we stop a flag it files from becoming a rule we tune to satisfy it?

6. Identity. Every measurement limit this year has bottomed out in the same place: cookie profiles cannot be re-contacted, cannot be verified, vanish, and 12% of them are ghosts. The nudge, the study, the three-week follow-up and any longitudinal claim all need it. Q1 refused PII for good reasons that still hold. Q4 has to decide whether the transfer question is worth an account system, and it should decide that directly rather than arriving at it through a feature.

7. The library puts its trouble on the left 17 times in 26, and all six recovery puzzles are left. Two holes moved that to roughly 60/40. Is a balanced library reachable at any affordable price, or should lateral-bias be permanently scoped to the approach class and honestly labelled as such? A player-model claim that only works for one of four categories is a smaller product than the one we described when we started building it.

8. merion-11 introduced a category we did not have: content that is correct and below the instrument's resolution. A 5-yard brook against GRID_SPACING_YDS of 6 and floors.anchor of 0.08 at 600 samples. There are real holes whose whole character is a hazard this engine cannot see, and raising MC_SAMPLES or tightening the grid trades directly against the 900ms reveal and the note's p99. That is a genuine ceiling on what this product can ever be about, and we should write it down as one rather than rediscovering it per hole.

## Q4, year one — Consolidation, and What the Note Is Actually For

Three quarters built an apparatus: a ledger, two golden corpora, a drop log, a transfer probe, a pattern registry, an exclusion job, twelve rules and their tests. Q4's job was to pay for it. We ran the census the previous quarters kept postponing — not "which rules fire" but "what does each rule cost to keep true, and what is its number actually made of" — and it returned two things we did not want. First, the load-bearing numeral in two of our rules is not measured at all: `lie-widens` prints `LIE_SIGMA_MULTIPLIER[lie]` verbatim, and `club-clamp`'s carry number is `DRIVER_CARRY_PER_MPH × clubSpeedMph × CLUB_GAPPING.fraction` — arithmetic over two placeholder constants and a self-reported club speed, underlined in flag red as the one thing the player should take away. The constants file says of itself "reasonable placeholders seeded from the product spec — tune freely." For a year we have been claiming every sentence is entailed by a number the engine computed, and some of those numbers are numbers we typed. Second, Q3 assigned Q4 the novel-geometry test, and we ran it: on `firestone-16` and `bethpage-4`, held out of the queue until a player exhausted the other 40 puzzles, cross-hole `movedToward` on genuinely unseen ground is 52% against a 50% null, and 49% in the over-18 group. Four quarters, four measurements, and the number has not left the null since we learned to decompose it. So Q4 shipped a provenance axis (SAMPLED / DERIVED / AUTHORED) with a CI rule that an AUTHORED numeral may never be the key token; a constants-drift gate that perturbs each engine constant ±20% and re-runs the corpus; a `constantsHash` on every ledger row, because `rulesVersion` tracked the sentences and nothing tracked the numbers underneath them; and silence — the note may now print nothing at all, and on 74% of Perfect notes it does. We took the registry from 12 rules to 9, cut the tendency card from four pattern rules to two after eligibility landed at 17% against Q3's own 40% test, refused a per-club carry override that the HeatmapCache key cannot carry, refused accounts for the fourth consecutive quarter, and rewrote the spec's vision paragraph to claim only what a year of evidence supports: this teaches the holes it contains. That last one is the uncomfortable thing we have been deferring since Q1, and it is a retraction, not a roadmap item.

### Shipped

**Silence — the note may print nothing** — `READ_BUDGET.perfect` becomes 0 when no SAMPLED-tier claim is admissible, and `fallback-strokes`' guard goes from the literal `() => true` to `band !== 'perfect' || sgLoss > floors.strokes`. On a Perfect where nothing measured is true, the reveal is the band stamp, the sgLoss, the isolines, the benchmark glyph and Q3's offset stake — and no sentence. `READ_BUDGET.okay` also drops 2 → 1, because after the deletions the READ side is four rules wide and stretching it re-inflated the backstop.

*Why:* Q3 measured Perfect-band dwell at 1.3s and `fallback-strokes` at 1.4s: the two least-read things in the product were the sentence we print when we have nothing to say and the band where we have nothing to say. The band budget has believed since launch that when the player was right the caddie says less. We finally followed it to zero.

*Cost:* The totality invariant `fallback-strokes` existed to prove — every input produces a sentence — is gone, replaced by a weaker one we had to state and then enforce twice (see failures): every reveal carries a sentence OR a mark, never neither. And 19% of production notes now print no text, which means 19% of notes are unscoreable by the transfer probe and invisible to dwell.

**Provenance: SAMPLED / DERIVED / AUTHORED on every rule** — A second axis alongside Q2's stake tier. SAMPLED means the numeral comes out of the 600-sample Monte Carlo (`featureHits[].fraction`, `onGreen.fraction`, `lieBreakdown`). DERIVED means arithmetic over sampled values (`player.e − correction.e`, delta-share). AUTHORED means it comes from `lib/engine/constants.ts` or from `data/holes/` — `LIE_SIGMA_MULTIPLIER`, the club table, a traced hole distance, a hazard name. A rule declares the provenance of every token it emits, and a CI assertion in `explain.test.ts` fails the build if a token marked `key: true` is AUTHORED.

*Why:* Q2's label leak was an AUTHORED noun reaching a player. Nobody asked the same question about the numerals. When we did, `lie-widens`' only number was a constant and `club-clamp`'s underlined number was a constant times a self-report.

*Cost:* Naming collision with the Q2 stake tier, which already uses MEASURED/FLOOR for a different thing; we chose SAMPLED/DERIVED/AUTHORED to keep them distinguishable and accept that two axes on one rule is more surface to keep straight. It also required a token-level annotation pass over all 12 rules — the tokens existed since launch, the labels did not.

**`npm run explain:drift` — the constants-perturbation gate** — Offline CI job. For each constant in `lib/engine/constants.ts`, perturb ±20%, regenerate grids (not fixtures — see failures), re-run the 282-note corpus, and report two columns: which rules' printed numerals move, and which rules' firing changes. Committed as `data/drift.json`, diffed in review like `data/fit.json`. `DEFAULT_SEED` is excluded by decision — perturbing it makes the corpus non-deterministic, which Q2 already refused.

*Why:* Every truth claim we have made for a year is entailment against numbers the file itself calls placeholders. If someone tunes `LATERAL_SIGMA_BY_HANDICAP` next year, we needed to know in advance which sentences change and which sentences stop existing.

*Cost:* 11m 20s in CI over 141 (puzzle, bucket) pairs × 9 perturbed constants; it runs nightly, not per-commit. And it produced the most alarming number of the quarter (see evidence), which we now have to carry into every constants change forever.

**`constantsHash` on NoteEvent and on HeatmapCache** — A short hash of `lib/engine/constants.ts` stamped on every ledger row and every cached grid. Analysis refuses to pool across differing hashes exactly as it already refuses to pool across `rulesVersion`. HeatmapCache rows whose hash does not match the running build are treated as misses.

*Why:* `rulesVersion` tracks the sentences. Nothing tracked the numbers the sentences are about. Q1 chose not to store rendered text because notes are deterministically reconstructable — but reconstruction requires the constants, and we were one tuning commit away from a ledger nobody could re-derive.

*Cost:* A constants change now invalidates the whole heatmap cache, which is the 900ms reveal's only protection. We measured the recovery: a full cache cold-start across 141 pairs is ~19 minutes of background worker time, during which the fallback-path share rises from 2.1% to roughly 30% and the reveal budget is genuinely at risk. Tuning a constant is now an operational event, and that is the honest cost of having pretended it wasn't one.

**Registry 12 → 9: `lie-widens` and `depth-error` deleted, `free-gain` merged into `cheap-bail`** — `lie-widens` deleted — its key numeral is an AUTHORED constant and cannot be fixed (see failures). `depth-error` deleted — its claim is a carry error in an engine where carry equals total, so 'the number was 20 yards short' is 20 yards of carry on a shot the model never rolls. `free-gain` folded into `cheap-bail` as a second branch on the same chosen correction: one rule, one offset, one percentage, either the trouble falling or the green holding, never both. The merged rule keeps the id `cheap-bail`.

*Why:* Every one of these was carrying maintenance we could name: 1,180 lines of rule code and rule tests entering the quarter, and a rule census that priced each one. Keeping `cheap-bail` as the id was deliberate — four quarters of transfer data on that id stays poolable, at the price of an id that is now wrong for half its branches.

*Cost:* The READ slot is four rules wide and one of them is a backstop. The `depth` tag is empty, and the layup puzzles — which Q2 told to lean on `depth-error` and `aim-line` after it cut `leave` — now lean on `aim-line` alone. That is two rules removed from the four layup situations in two quarters, and layups are the category we bought a hole trace for.

**The hazard stake — the READ drawn on the map** — Q3's offset stake drew the MOVE. This draws the READ: the polygon the merged hazard rule names gets a hairline callout at its hit locus during the reveal's second beat, with its percentage in mono. Same numeral, same `pct()` call, same floors. No new engine work — `FeatureHit.locus` has been in `OutcomeStats` since launch and was only ever used for the label's position phrase.

*Why:* The offset stake is the only intervention this product has produced that moved a number (54% → 59% on sub-1.2s-dwell notes). Its mechanism — put the claim where a 1.2-second look lands — applies to the READ exactly as well as to the MOVE, and we had one quarter's evidence and no reason to wait.

*Cost:* It did not replicate cleanly (see evidence). And it is a second surface that must agree with the sentence, so a rule that stands down on verify() now has to also suppress a drawn callout; we found one ordering bug in review where the callout drew for a claim the numeral guard had dropped.

**Cross-hole transfer as the reported metric; same-hole relabelled recall** — The transfer probe's situation class is unchanged, but the reported figure is now cross-hole only. Same-hole re-encounters are reported separately, under the name recall, in every place the number appears. The pooled figure is not computed anywhere.

*Why:* Q3 found the pooled 58% was substantially players remembering a hole, and predicted we would keep quoting the flattering number if we left it available. We had done exactly that for two quarters. The fix is to delete the flattering number, not to footnote it.

*Cost:* Four quarters of headline become non-comparable to what we report now, and the honest restatement is that the product's teaching metric has never been above 53%.

**The corpus reading rota, and the critic pass as a build step that cannot fail the build** — A `rulesVersion` bump now requires `corpus-read.json`: a human's initials against a hash of the corpus and a count of notes actually read, minimum 40, sampled across buckets and bands rather than chosen. The Q3 LLM critic pass runs in CI on every corpus change and files flags into `data/critic-flags.json` for triage; it is advisory by construction and cannot fail a build.

*Why:* Q3 open question five: verify() proves the numeral, not its referent, and the only instrument that catches a misreadable sentence is a human reading the corpus — which nobody did more than once a quarter. The rota caught a real defect in its first month (see failures).

*Cost:* Q2's own lesson was that warnings which ship are not checks, and we have just shipped a warning on purpose. The argument for keeping the critic non-blocking is Q3's: a flag that can fail the build becomes a rule we tune to satisfy a model. We are aware that is the same argument we rejected for the content audit, and the difference we are claiming is that the audit checks a fact and the critic checks a reading.

### The evidence

THE NOVEL-GEOMETRY TEST, which Q3 assigned as Q4's first job. `firestone-16` and `bethpage-4` shipped in Q3 week 10 and we held all 7 of their puzzles out of `pickNext`'s candidate list until a profile had played all 40 of the older ones. That makes them a test set rather than content: a player meets them only after exhausting everything else, so a same-class re-encounter on one of them is an encounter on ground they have provably never seen. 486 such re-encounters across 121 profiles. `movedToward` 52%, 95% interval 47.6–56.4. Over-18 subgroup 49% (n=171). By rule: `cheap-bail` 55% (n=163), `aim-line` 50% (n=141), `hold-the-line` 51% (n=96). The rest of the quarter's cross-hole population (older holes, unseen class-transfer) sits at 53% (n=1,880), unchanged from Q3's 53%. The four-quarter series is 58 pooled, 58 pooled, 58 pooled / 53 cross-hole, 53 cross-hole / 52 novel-hole. Two holes is two holes, both are par 5s, and `bethpage-4`'s trouble is right off the tee, which is the side the library is short of — so the test set is not representative of anything except itself. But the direction has not moved once in a year, and every time we have sharpened the measure the number has fallen toward the null.

THE DRIFT CENSUS, and it is the finding of the quarter. Perturbing each constant ±20% and re-running 282 corpus notes:

- `LIE_SIGMA_MULTIPLIER` (1.25 rough / 1.5 sand / 1.4 recovery): `lie-widens`' printed numeral moves one-for-one on every note it fires — 84 of 282, 14 of 47 puzzles. It is the constant. No other rule's numeral moves. Firing changes: zero.
- `LATERAL_SIGMA_BY_HANDICAP`: 22 of 282 notes flip between `sub-resolution` and `aim-line`. Both guards test `Math.hypot(toOptimal.lat, toOptimal.long)` against `Math.max(6, facts.sigmaLat)`, so the two rules are the two sides of one threshold — one says *there is nothing here to correct*, the other says *aim 14 yards left*. At h5 the guard is the literal 6 (0.038 × 150y = 5.7y, under the floor), so the drift is entirely at h15 and h30, and at h30 `sigmaLat` is linear extrapolation past the table's last anchor at handicap 20. The most consequential instruction in the product — do something versus do nothing, aimed at the players whose dispersion is widest — turns on an extrapolated placeholder, and a 20% change in it rewrites 7.8% of notes.
- `HANDICAP_MULTIPLIER_PER_STROKE` (0.011): band changes on 9 of 282 (3.2%). Band drives `READ_BUDGET`, so a placeholder constant sets how many sentences a note has.
- `MARGINAL_COST.bunker` 0.45 → 0.54: the merged hazard rule names a different polygon on 6 of 47 puzzles at h15. This table is documented "ordering weights only — never printed," which is true and beside the point: it does not print, it chooses which true thing prints.
- `WATER_PENALTY` 1 → 1.2: hazard driver changes on 4 of 47; `chooseCorrection` returns a different probe on 3, because its `goodThreshold` of 0.1 is itself a constant sitting next to `SCORE_BANDS[1].maxSgLoss` of 0.1 with no code path connecting them.
- `MC_SAMPLES` 600 → 300 (not a tuning, a budget decision): `floors.anchor` 0.08 → 0.113 and `floors.print` 0.05 → 0.071, and the merged hazard rule stands down on 11 of 47 puzzles at h5. Our honesty floor is calibrated to a sample count chosen to fit the reveal.
- `GRID_SPACING_YDS` 6 → 5: `hold-the-line`'s printed corridor width changes by 3 yards or more on 9 of 47, because the corridor is a lattice walk at cell resolution.

Six of twelve rules print a numeral that moves under a ±20% change in some constant. Two print a numeral that *is* one.

THE CLUB-CLAMP FINDING, which the provenance pass turned up and which is worse than `lie-widens`. `club-clamp` is the h5 bucket's most frequent READ (44% after Q3 deleted `green-rate`) and fires on 11 of 26 comparable puzzles at h30. Its key token — the one underlined in flag red — is `yardsMeasured(facts.playerAimDistance)`, which on a clamped shot is `maxCarry`, which is `DRIVER_CARRY_PER_MPH (2.45) × profile.clubSpeedMph × CLUB_GAPPING[club].fraction`. No Monte Carlo touches it. It is two placeholders multiplied by a number the player typed into `/settings`, printed back to them as a fact about their own golf swing, and it is the single sentence in this product most likely to be read by someone who knows it is wrong. At 110 mph the model gives a 7 iron 189 yards, which is a tour carry, and 62 of our 262 profiles declared a club speed at or above 110.

THE COST CENSUS — what each rule costs to keep true. Rule code plus rule tests, defects it caused this year, field firing share, dwell, cross-hole transfer. Entering Q4: 1,180 lines across `rules.ts` and `explain.test.ts`. `hazard` (merged) 96 + 210 lines, 2 defects (Q2 verify driver mismatch, Q2 label leak), 21% firing, 2.9s dwell. `fallback-strokes` 18 + 44, 2 defects (Q1's fabricated 0.03, Q4's blank Perfect), 3.1%, 1.4s. `club-down` 24 + 88, 1 defect, 4.4%, formally unmeasured since Q3. `lie-widens` 19 + 52, 2 defects (Q2's declared-versus-classified lie, Q4's constant laundering), 6.1%, 2.1s. `depth-error` 31 + 96, 1 critic flag plus the roll-out finding, 4.0%, cross-hole 51%. `sub-resolution` 22 + 60, zero defects and the most drift-sensitive rule in the file. Leaving Q4: 870 lines. The census is the argument for the deletions, and the thing it makes legible is that defect count does not track firing share — `fallback-strokes` fires on 3.1% of notes and has caused two of the year's five field truth escapes, both times by being the rule that speaks when nothing else can.

SILENCE, measured honestly. Shipped week 3. In the post-ship window, 19% of production notes print no sentence at all; on the Perfect band, 74%. What moved: median dwell on notes that *do* print went 2.4s → 2.9s, and the under-1.2s share past a player's 40th attempt went 47% → 39%. Both of those are composition, not attention — we deleted the 1.3s notes, so the mean of what remains went up, and we are stating that as arithmetic rather than as a result. What did not move: notes per session 11.4 → 11.7, inside week-to-week variance. Re-aim (practice) rate after a silent Perfect 8.1% against 7.6% after a printed Perfect, inside noise once the fallback-path defect was fixed. Next-attempt sgLoss, unchanged. The defensible summary is that we removed 19% of the sentences this product says and nothing detectable got worse, which is the strongest available argument that those sentences were not doing anything — and is also exactly what you would see if the notes were not doing anything either.

THE HAZARD STAKE DID NOT REPLICATE THE OFFSET STAKE. Shipped week 6, alone in its build, same pre/post design as Q3's and the same restriction to players active in both windows (n=104). On sub-1.2s-dwell notes carrying a hazard READ, `movedToward` went 57% (n=421) to 58% (n=466). Interval spans zero. Q3's offset stake moved 5 points on the same design; this moved 1. The difference we can name is that the offset stake draws an *instruction* — a leader line to a point you can aim at — and the hazard callout draws a *diagnosis*, a percentage next to a pond. If that reading is right it is the same finding as the whole year in miniature: the thing that changes an aim is a place to aim, and everything else is commentary. We are not confident enough in it to have redesigned anything.

THE 500-NOTE PLAYER. 11 profiles have passed 500 recorded notes. Their mean distinct rules across their last 10 notes is 5.8 against the cohort's 6.4. Median dwell 1.1s, with 71% of notes under 1.2s. Recall 56%, cross-hole 51%. Of each of their last 100 notes, a median of 62 were on a puzzle they had played three or more times — `pickNext` returns `repeat: true` and re-serves the least-recently-played puzzle, `DEFAULT_SEED` is fixed, the grid comes from `HeatmapCache`, and the bucket has not changed, so a re-aim within a yard is still a literal replay. What Q4 shipped for these players is silence: 34% of their notes are Perfect against the cohort's 21%, so they are disproportionately the people the silence rule serves. That is not an answer to the 500th note. It is the product getting quieter as the player gets better, which is the band budget's belief and not a curriculum.

THE TENDENCY CARD, against Q3's own test. Q3 said: if eligibility reaches 40%, this was infrastructure; if it stays under 20%, it was a beautiful thing built for almost nobody. 262 active profiles, 44 eligible, 17%. The library grew 40 → 47 and the distinct-puzzle gate loosened as predicted; the both-side hazard gate did not, because the two new holes moved the library from 26 left / 14 right to 28 left / 19 right and the recovery class is still 6 / 0. `lateral-bias` is still ineligible in recovery by construction and now permanently scoped to approach by decision. Among the 44: `lateral-bias` 26, `clamp-habit` 21, `hazard-cost` 14, `depth-bias` 7. `clamp-habit` remains the one that says something a table cannot — 'on 14 of your last 38 tee shots you aimed past your own driver carry' — and it is also, after the provenance finding, a claim built on the same authored carry number as `club-clamp`. We kept it and moved its key token to the count.

BUDGET. Field `noteMs`: p50 27ms (was 31), p95 104ms (was 118), p99 236ms (was 251), against roughly 250ms of stamp-beat headroom. For the first time in four quarters the p99 is inside the budget, and it is not an achievement — it is three fewer guards and one fewer claim build, worth about 6%, and the 8,400 Monte Carlo samples (LADDER_LAT 8 + LADDER_LONG 5 + one `evaluateAim` × 600) are untouched and still dominate. A margin of 14ms is a coincidence, not headroom: one more rule puts us back outside. Cached-grid share 97.9%; the 2.1% fallback path still pays the grid's median 721ms / p95 1.84s and is still the only genuine risk to the 900ms reveal. `content:fit` 5m 20s. `explain:drift` 11m 20s, nightly.

### What broke

- **The silence rule made the ledger silent. `components/puzzle/PuzzleScreen.tsx` posts the NoteEvent row from the note's `ruleIds`, and the posting guard treated an empty array as 'no note was generated' — the same shape the degraded path used to produce. So from week 3 to week 6, every silent note recorded nothing: no row, no dwell sample, no band, no entry in the drop log. The Perfect stratum went dark in the one quarter we were trying to measure what happens when we say nothing.**
  - Caught by: Not by an error — by a ratio. The notes-per-recorded-attempt figure, which had sat at 0.98 for two quarters, fell to 0.86 within a day of the week-3 deploy while NoteEvent orphan rate stayed flat at 1.4%. Orphan rate is the check we had; it cannot see a row that was never written.
  - Response: NoteEvent gains `silent: boolean` and is emitted for every reveal including empty ones, with `ruleIds: []` meaning exactly that and never meaning 'nothing happened'. The band mix and dwell figures for weeks 3–6 are re-derived from Attempt rows, which carry band and sgLoss but no rule information, so that window can answer 'how often was it silent' and cannot answer 'what did the non-silent ones say'. Cost: three weeks of the Perfect band, in the quarter whose central intervention was about the Perfect band, and a reminder that Q1 built the ledger to be the receipt for the note and never made it the receipt for the note's absence.
- **Blank Perfect on the worker-fallback path — a reveal with no sentence and no mark. On the 2.1% of notes where `HeatmapCache` misses, `grid` is null, so `correction`, `corridor` and `toOptimal` are all null, no MOVE rule can fire, `mark` is null by construction (`correction && move`), and every READ rule except the backstop stands down. Narrowing `fallback-strokes` to skip Perfect meant those reveals printed the band stamp and nothing else — no sentence, no callout, no leader line. 340 notes over 11 days.**
  - Caught by: dwellMs. Blank Perfect reveals had a median dwell of 0.6s against 1.3s for printed Perfects, with a 4× rate of immediate re-aim. The instrument Q3 built to ask whether notes were read turned out to be a decent instrument for whether the screen looked broken.
  - Response: The totality invariant was restated and then enforced: every reveal carries a sentence or a mark, never neither, asserted in `explain.test.ts` over the degraded-path fixture that had existed since launch and only ever tested that a note came back non-null. `fallback-strokes` now fires on Perfect when `grid === null`, which is the one case where silence has nothing to stand next to. Cost: 340 reveals that read as a failed render, and the uncomfortable structural point that we deleted a sentence on the strength of what the *map* was carrying without checking the path where the map carries nothing. Silence is only honest when something else is speaking.
- **We tried to fix `lie-widens` by measuring it, and measured the same constant. The rule prints `LIE_SIGMA_MULTIPLIER[lie]` — '1.25× as wide as it does from grass' — so the obvious repair was to replace the literal with the observed ratio of the player's lateral sigma from this lie against fairway, taken from `dispersionParams()`. We shipped that to a branch and the golden corpus diff came back byte-identical across all 282 notes.**
  - Caught by: The corpus diff. `dispersionParams` computes sigmaLat as `lateralSigmaFraction(handicap) × distance × LIE_SIGMA_MULTIPLIER[lie]`, so the ratio of the two calls is the constant, exactly, with the distance and handicap terms cancelling. The 'measured' version was the same number laundered through one function call and a division.
  - Response: The branch was abandoned and `lie-widens` deleted (see cuts). Cost: two days, and a lesson that generalises past this rule — you cannot measure your way out of a constant when the measurement is defined in terms of it. It also retroactively indicts the fix we made in Q2, where we made `lie-widens` guard on the classified lie agreeing with the declared one. That fix made the rule stop firing on bad content; it did not make the number it prints any more real, and we called it fixed.
- **The drift gate's first run was green for the constant that matters most, because it was perturbing a constant the corpus had already baked into a fixture. `explain-golden.ts` reads committed grid fixtures for speed; `facts.sigmaLat` comes from `grid.brief`, which is inside the fixture. So a ±40% perturbation of `LATERAL_SIGMA_BY_HANDICAP` changed nothing in the corpus, and the first drift census reported `sub-resolution` and `aim-line` as insensitive — the opposite of the truth.**
  - Caught by: By disbelief, not by a check. A reviewer noted that a rule whose guard literally reads `Math.max(6, facts.sigmaLat)` cannot be insensitive to the table that produces `sigmaLat`, and traced it to the fixture. Nothing in CI would ever have caught it, because a gate that reports 'no drift' looks exactly like a gate that is working.
  - Response: `explain:drift` now regenerates grids from geometry rather than reading fixtures, which is what took it from 90 seconds to 11m 20s and moved it to a nightly job. Cost: four hours of CI time re-running the census, and the wrong version is the one that was presented at the mid-quarter review and briefly reassured everyone. This is the second consecutive quarter in which an instrument arrived carrying the assumption it was built to test — Q3's dwell counter over-counted in the direction that flattered us, and this one under-reported drift in the direction that flattered us. We do not have a process fix for that. We have a habit of checking the flattering result first, and it has now paid twice.
- **The merged `cheap-bail` printed two percentages in one sentence for nine days. Folding `free-gain` in gave the rule two branches — trouble falling, green holding — and on three puzzles both were true and the claim builder emitted both: 'Aim 12 yards left of that and the trouble falls to 9%, and the green holds 61% of the pattern.' Q2's numeral guard permitted it, correctly by its own rule, because 9% and 61% are further apart than `floors.print`. Every word entailed. 96 notes, 41 players.**
  - Caught by: The corpus reading rota, on its first pass. A human read 40 sampled notes for a `rulesVersion` bump and wrote 'this is `room` again' in the sign-off — which is exactly right: two numbers about two different things inside one short note is the defect that killed `room` in Q1 and that Q2 refused to reintroduce when it declined to split the hazard tags. We had rebuilt it inside a single sentence, where the tag-diversity check and the numeral guard both look straight past it.
  - Response: The merged rule now selects one branch by stake and prints one percentage. Cost: nine days live, and an argument we have to keep making — the numeral guard enforces 'one number, one claim' at the note level and has no concept of a claim saying two things at once. The wider point is the one the rota was built for: three CI gates and a verify() function all passed a sentence that a person recognised as a known mistake in one reading. We are counting this in the truth column even though it violates no entailment, because the alternative is a definition of truth that we get to grade ourselves against.

### Decisions and roads not taken

- **Let the note be silent. On a Perfect with no SAMPLED-tier claim admissible, print no sentence — the stamp, the field, the benchmark and the offset stake carry the reveal.**
  - Q3 measured the Perfect note at 1.3s dwell and `fallback-strokes` at 1.4s, the two lowest numbers in the product. The band budget has said since launch that the note should shrink with the size of the mistake; zero is where that belief ends and we had never gone there. The evidence after shipping is that nothing detectable got worse and 19% of our sentences stopped existing.
  - Rejected: Keeping `fallback-strokes` as a universal backstop for the sake of the totality property — rejected because totality was a property of the architecture, not a service to the player, and 'every input produces a sentence' had become a reason to print a sentence with nothing in it. A one-line affirmation on Perfect ('that is the line') — rejected on `BANNED_LEXICON` in `format.ts`, which contains 'great', 'nice', 'well done'; the system has no praise vocabulary by construction and adding one for the band where we have least to say is the wrong place to start. A shortest-possible note instead of no note — rejected because it is the same 1.3s sentence with fewer words, and the measurement we wanted was of absence, not of brevity.
- **Declare provenance on every token and forbid an AUTHORED numeral from being the key token.**
  - The product's whole claim is that the underlined number is entailed by something the engine computed. `lie-widens` underlined a constant and `club-clamp` underlined two constants times a self-report. A rule that says which of its numbers came from the Monte Carlo and which came from the file is the cheapest possible check on the one promise we make.
  - Rejected: Deleting every rule with any authored input — rejected as far too wide: `forced-carry`'s stake is scaled by a literal 0.5 and its printed numerals are both sampled shares, and the stake is an ordering weight nobody sees. Provenance is a property of what is printed, not of what is computed. A blanket attribution phrase ('in this model, your 7 iron carries…') — rejected on the same ground as every hedge we have refused for three quarters: `BANNED_LEXICON` has no hedging adverbs because the floors are the hedge, and an attribution clause on one sentence implies the others do not need one. Keeping `club-clamp`'s carry as the key token with a footnote — rejected because a footnote is a hedge with extra steps.
- **Refuse a per-club carry override in settings, and say plainly why.**
  - This is the most tempting feature of the quarter and it dies on a constraint. `HeatmapCache` is keyed on `(puzzleId, profileBucket)` where the bucket is handicap-to-5, speed-to-10, shape. A per-club carry table is thirteen more dimensions on that key; the cache stops hitting; the 97.9% cached-grid share collapses toward the fallback path, which pays a median 721ms and a p95 of 1.84s for the grid, and the 900ms reveal is gone. So: the one number in the note that the player knows better than we do is the one number we will not let them correct, because the cache key cannot carry it. What we did instead is free — `club-clamp`'s key token moved to the shortfall, and `/settings` now displays the derived carry table, so the number in the note appears somewhere the player can see where it came from.
  - Rejected: A single global carry-scale multiplier, which would fit in the bucket key — rejected because it moves every club together and the actual complaint is gapping, so it would let a player make the driver right and every iron wrong. Manual club selection at aim time — out of v1 scope by the spec, and it changes what a puzzle *is*: the aim is the decision, and choosing the club for the aim is the engine's job. Computing the carry table from the player's own recorded attempts — rejected because attempts record aims, not shots; we have never observed a single golf ball.
- **Delete `depth-error` on the grounds that its claim is entailed by a documented simplification rather than by the hole.**
  - Carry equals total in this engine — no roll-out, stated in the engine README and the spec. `depth-error` prints 'the line was right; the number was 20 yards short', where 20 yards is a carry offset on a shot the model never rolls. On a real 150-yard approach a ball carrying 20 short finishes materially less than 20 short, so the rule systematically overstates by an amount the engine cannot know. Q3's critic pass had already flagged the sentence as ambiguous between the aim and the outcome; investigating the ambiguity found the deeper problem.
  - Rejected: Rewording to make the referent explicit ('your aim was 20 yards short of the best one') — rejected because it fixes the ambiguity and leaves the overstatement, and the overstatement is the defect. Adding a roll-out model — rejected outright: it is an engine change, it invalidates every baseline anchor and every cached grid, and it is the exact scope Q1 declined when it kept `leave` out of a one-shot engine (before Q2 deleted `leave` for the same reason). Scoping the rule to layups where roll matters least — rejected because layups are precisely where it was still speaking after `leave` went, so scoping it there keeps the rule alive exactly where the claim is doing the most work.
- **Report cross-hole transfer only; retire the pooled figure entirely; call same-hole re-encounters recall.**
  - Q3 decomposed the probe and predicted, from evidence, that leaving the pooled number available meant we would keep quoting it. We had done that for two quarters. Deleting the computation is the only version of this decision that binds.
  - Rejected: Keeping pooled with the split as a standing footnote — rejected for the reason above, which is not a hypothesis about us but an observation of us. Redefining the situation class to include `holeId` — rejected again, same as Q3: it destroys the comparison that is the finding. Reporting recall as a success metric in its own right — rejected, and this one was contested. Remembering a hole is a real thing a training product can do, and a golfer who plays the same course every week is served by it. But we cannot serve that player: we have twelve holes, none of which is their course, and calling recall a win would be measuring the product's ability to teach its own furniture.
- **Cut the tendency card from four pattern rules to two and scope `lateral-bias` to the approach category permanently.**
  - Q3 set the test itself: over 40% eligibility means infrastructure, under 20% means a beautiful thing built for almost nobody. It came in at 17% (44 of 262). We held to the test rather than relitigating it. `hazard-cost` and `depth-bias` go; `lateral-bias` and `clamp-habit` stay, because `clamp-habit` is the only pattern claim that has ever told a player something the `/summary` tables do not already contain, and `lateral-bias` is the one the both-side gate was built for.
  - Rejected: Deleting the whole surface and the pattern registry with it — rejected, narrowly. The registry is the only place in the system where a claim is computed off the 250ms budget with a sign test behind it, and the pocket card (41 profiles in Q3, 58 now) is generated from it. Killing it would also kill the one artefact that leaves the product. Loosening the both-side hazard gate to reach more players — rejected: the gate exists because the library puts its trouble left 28 times in 47, and loosening it would make 'your aim sits right of the best line' a statement about our tracing. Waiting one more quarter for the library to balance — rejected because balancing requires traces and we cut the traces.
- **Refuse to raise `MC_SAMPLES` or tighten `GRID_SPACING_YDS`, and write the resulting ceiling down as a permanent property of the product rather than rediscovering it per hole.**
  - `merion-11` is benched because a 5-yard brook falls between 6-yard grid cells and catches 2.6% of a pattern against a 0.08 anchor floor. The fixes are more samples or a finer grid, and both trade directly against the 8,400 samples per note, the 236ms p99 and the 900ms reveal. Written down: this engine cannot say anything about a hazard narrower than its grid spacing or thinner than its floors, and there are real holes whose entire character is such a hazard. That is a ceiling on what SG Trainer can ever be about, not a backlog item.
  - Rejected: Adaptive sampling — denser near hazard boundaries — rejected because it makes the sample count vary per aim, which makes `floorsFor(nSamples)` vary per aim, which means two notes on the same hole print percentages at different honesty floors and the ledger pools them. Per-hole `nSamples` overrides — rejected for the same reason plus a worse one: the override would live in `data/holes/`, so a content author would be setting the product's truth threshold. Raising samples only for the offline `content:fit` job so exclusion is better resolved — this one we accepted, at 1,200 samples, because it is offline and its output is a sign, not a printed number.
- **No accounts, for the fourth consecutive quarter — and instead of deferring the transfer question again, rewrite the spec's claim to match the evidence.**
  - This is the strategic thing we have been deferring since Q1 and Q3 said to decide directly. The spec's vision paragraph says the product 'trains the fastest scoring gains available to a mid-handicap player'. After a year: cross-hole 53%, novel-hole 52%, over-18 cross-hole 49%, and nothing we have measured touches a score on a card. The choice was to buy the evidence — accounts, a paid cohort, a launch monitor so `LATERAL_SIGMA_BY_HANDICAP` stops being extrapolated past handicap 20 — or to stop making the claim. We stopped making the claim. The vision paragraph now says the product trains target selection on the holes it contains, and that transfer to unseen holes is unproven and measured at near chance. The paragraph that describes the reveal is unchanged, because the reveal is the part we can defend.
  - Rejected: An email-only, opt-in account for a study cohort — the closest call of the year, rejected on the Q3 grounds that optional PII is still PII, still a consent surface, still a breach we could have, and now on one more: an account would let us measure the claim, and we have four quarters of evidence about which direction the measurement moves each time we sharpen it. Buying evidence at that price to confirm a number that has fallen every time we looked harder is not a good trade. A paid 30-player study with scorecards and no accounts, run manually over a season — rejected on cost and on the confound that a paid cohort playing a trainer knows what it is being measured for. Keeping the spec sentence and calling it aspiration — rejected because it is the sentence a new engineer reads to learn what the product is for, and an aspiration in that position is a spec.

### Cut

Five things, and one of them is a sentence in the spec.

THE `lie-widens` RULE — deleted, registry 12 to 11. Its key numeral is `LIE_SIGMA_MULTIPLIER[facts.lie]`, printed verbatim: '1.25× as wide as it does from grass'. That is not a fact about the rough the player is standing in; it is a fact about a line in `constants.ts` that the file's own header calls a reasonable placeholder. We tried to measure it and measured the same constant laundered through `dispersionParams` (see failures), which is when we accepted it is not fixable — the multiplier is an input to the model, so no output of the model can independently confirm it. Cost, and it is a bill from two quarters ago: Q2's situation wave was bought partly to make this rule reachable, taking it from 4 of 26 puzzles to 13 of 40, and Q2 reported its firing pattern as 'the novelty governor's first legible win'. We paid for the reach and then deleted the destination. Rough, sand and recovery puzzles — 18 of 47 — now have no lie-specific sentence at all, and the widened dispersion is still in every number they do print, silently, which is where it always belonged.

THE `depth-error` RULE — deleted, registry 11 to 10 (then 10 to 9 with the `free-gain` merge). Killed for the reason Q2 killed `leave`: it is a claim about a shot this engine does not model. Carry equals total, there is no roll-out, so '20 yards short' is 20 yards of carry on a ball that in reality rolls, and the rule overstates by an amount the engine cannot compute and therefore cannot floor. Q3's critic pass flagged the sentence as ambiguous between the aim and the outcome and we logged it unfixed; Q4 went looking at the ambiguity and found the arithmetic underneath. Cost, and it compounds: the `depth` tag is empty, approach notes at h5 lost their second READ (which is part of why `READ_BUDGET.okay` came down to 1), and the layup category — `pebble-18-layup-2`, `standrews-17-layup-3`, the two from Q2's wave and the two real ones we bought a trace for in Q3 — now has `aim-line` and nothing else. Q2 cut `leave` and wrote that layups would lean on `depth-error` and `aim-line`. Q4 took the other one. The one category we spent money on has the thinnest rule coverage in the product, and that is a direct consequence of two individually-correct deletions.

THE TENDENCY CARD, HALVED — pattern registry 4 to 2, `hazard-cost` and `depth-bias` deleted, `lateral-bias` permanently scoped to the approach category. Q3 set the test — 40% eligibility and it was infrastructure, under 20% and it was a beautiful thing built for almost nobody — and Q4 measured 17%, 44 of 262. We held to Q3's number rather than arguing that the gates would loosen eventually, because they did loosen (the library grew, the distinct-puzzle gate relaxed) and eligibility still did not move: the binding constraint is the both-side hazard gate, and that is a property of a library whose trouble is left 28 times in 47 and whose entire recovery class is left. Cost: two claims that fired for 21 of the 44 are gone, and we have now built, gated, corpus-tested and half-deleted a surface inside three quarters. The honest read is that the pattern registry was the right architecture for a claim the content cannot yet support.

THE TEN-HOLE CONTENT WAVE — cut for the fourth consecutive quarter, and this time the argument against it changed. Q1 cut it because a bigger library rated at one bucket is a longer wrong-ordered queue. Q2 cut it on cost after exclusion dissolved that objection. Q3 bought two holes for hazard side and cut the wave again. Q4 cuts it because the novel-geometry test came back at 52%: more holes buys exhaustion relief and recall, and there is no evidence it buys transfer, which is the thing the spec claimed. Roughly six hours per trace plus the review burden that has caught a 13.6-acre fairway, a bunker over a swimming pool, a hazard the classifier silently swallowed, five rough lies the geometry contradicted, and one hole (`merion-11`) that was traced correctly and is below the instrument's resolution. Cost: the library is 47 puzzles against a median session of 11, 22 profiles hit review-by-recency in Q1 and far more have now, `riviera-10` has been benched for four quarters against imagery that has not changed, and the side-bias problem that makes `lateral-bias` approach-only is a content problem we have now declined to fix four times.

THE SPEC'S TRANSFER CLAIM — cut, and this is the largest thing in the quarter. `docs/spec.md`'s vision paragraph has said since before the engine existed that the product 'trains target selection and course management — the fastest scoring gains available to a mid-handicap player — the way tactics puzzles train chess pattern recognition.' A year of measurement says: cross-hole 53%, novel-hole 52%, over-18 cross-hole 49% against a 50% null, no sgLoss improvement we can resolve above a ±0.01–0.02 Monte Carlo standard error, and not one observation of a golf ball. The paragraph now claims what we can defend — that it trains target selection on the holes it contains, and that transfer to unseen holes is unproven and measures at near chance — and the reveal's paragraph is untouched, because the reveal is the part that survived. We considered buying the evidence instead: an opt-in email account, a paid cohort, a launch monitor to stop extrapolating `LATERAL_SIGMA_BY_HANDICAP` past handicap 20. We refused, for the reasons Q1 and Q3 gave and one new one — every time in four quarters that we have sharpened this measurement, the number has moved toward the null, and paying for identity to sharpen it once more is a bet against our own evidence. Cost, stated plainly: the product's most ambitious sentence is gone, the thing that replaces it is smaller than what anyone signed up to build, and if that sentence was the reason the feature existed then this quarter is the one where we wrote down that it does not do the thing. We would rather carry that in the spec than in the marketing.

### Still open

1. Is the note the product, or the caption? Two quarters of intervention evidence point the same way: the offset stake — a leader line to a point, drawn on the map — moved `movedToward` 5 points on skimmed notes, and every text intervention we have shipped has moved nothing measurable. The hazard callout, which draws a diagnosis rather than an instruction, moved 1 point. If the reveal's geometry is the teacher and the sentence is a caption, then the right year-two shape is a much smaller rule set feeding a much richer drawing, and we have spent a year optimising the wrong artefact. We are not confident enough to act on that, and it is now the first thing to test rather than the last.

2. What does the product owe a player at their 500th note? We have 11 of them, they read at 1.1s, 62 of their last 100 notes are on puzzles they have played three or more times, and this quarter's answer to them was to say less. Silence is right and it is not a curriculum. The candidate answers are all expensive: a much larger library (traces we keep cutting), per-bucket ratings so the ordering stops being wrong for them (deferred four quarters), or a different mode entirely for players past exhaustion — which is a new product, not a rule.

3. What happens the first time someone actually tunes a constant? We now have `constantsHash` so the ledger will not lie about it, and `explain:drift` so we will know what changes. What we do not have is a policy: a tuning invalidates every HeatmapCache row (~19 minutes of cold start, fallback share to ~30%, the 900ms reveal genuinely at risk during it), splits the ledger into two unpoolable halves, and — per the drift census — rewrites up to 7.8% of notes' instructions. Is the honest position that engine constants are frozen for v1, and if so, does that make `LATERAL_SIGMA_BY_HANDICAP`'s extrapolation past handicap 20 permanent?

4. `club-clamp` survived the provenance rule by moving its key token off the carry number, and the carry number is still in the sentence. It is still two placeholders times a self-report, told to the person best placed to know it is wrong, on 44% of h5 READs. We refused the fix (the cache key cannot carry a per-club table). Year two either finds a fix that fits the bucket key, or accepts that the most-printed READ at the low end is the least defensible number in the product.

5. The READ side is four rules wide and one is a backstop. `depth`, `lie`, `green`, `room` and `leave` are all empty tags now, deleted one per quarter, each for a good reason. At what point is a note with one sentence drawn from four candidates not a rule system but a template with a switch? The census says the deletions were right individually. Nothing in the census says the endpoint is right.

6. Recall is 65% and we have decided not to count it. That decision rests on the library being twelve holes none of which is any player's home course. If year two ever traces a course rather than a set of famous finishing holes, recall stops being an artefact and starts being the product — and the thing we would then be building is a course-specific yardage-book trainer, which is a different business with a different content cost and a much clearer claim.

7. The instrument ceiling, written down in Q4 and now needing a decision rather than a note: `GRID_SPACING_YDS` 6, `MC_SAMPLES` 600, `floors.anchor` 0.08. Holes whose character is a narrow hazard — `merion-11`'s brook — are outside what this engine can discuss, and the ceiling is set by the 900ms reveal. Is there a version of this product where the grid is precomputed offline at a far finer resolution and the reveal never computes anything? That trades the worker fallback path away entirely and makes content authoring the whole cost, which may be the honest shape.

8. And the one behind all the others: with the spec's transfer claim retracted, what is the sentence that replaces it? 'Trains target selection on the holes it contains' is defensible and small. Year two either grows the claim by buying the evidence — accounts, a cohort, a launch monitor, all refused four times — or grows the product to fit the small claim honestly. What we should not do is leave the small sentence in the spec and keep building as though the large one were still there.

## SG Trainer — The Caddie's Note, Year One Retrospective

*Written for the people building year two. Read section 7 first if you only read one.*

---

## 1. THE VERDICT

**The explanation feature earned its place as an instrument and did not earn the claim we built it on: four quarters of green CI say the note is true, 53% cross-hole and 52% on novel ground say it does not teach beyond the holes it contains, and the only intervention that ever moved a player was a line drawn on a map.**

---

## 2. WHAT WE GOT RIGHT

**The decision that paid off most: making the note reconstructible and gating the build on a multi-profile corpus of its own output.** Not the rules. The instrument around them.

Q1 shipped two things that look like plumbing and were the whole year: a ledger that stores `ruleIds + rulesVersion + the key numeral` rather than rendered prose, and `scripts/explain-golden.ts` extended to 3 buckets × 2 aim modes as a build gate that fails on a 3-point frequency move. Because the sentence is a pure function of `(ruleId, rulesVersion, puzzleId, bucket, aim, DEFAULT_SEED)`, the ledger is a pointer into a deterministic function rather than a log.

The evidence that this was the right call, in order of how much it saved:

- **Every truth defect of the year was found by running the corpus, not by a user complaint.** `fallback-strokes` fabricating "0.03 strokes worse" at zero loss (6 of 26 puzzles, visible only in the h5 optimal-aim column — you have to be *good* to see it). `club-down` printing "140 yards instead of 140 yards" (h30 only; the collision needs a 6–9 yard gap over 100 yards). The Q2 hazard-rule verify/claim driver mismatch, caught by the backstop-share cap Q1 shipped for an unrelated reason. `merion-11` rejected *before ingest* because every note on all three buckets described the bunkers and never the brook.
- **Reconstruction let us price a failure exactly.** When `'far bank (off hole)'` — an authoring aside — leaked into player-facing sentences, we replayed every note that ever named that polygon: 187 notes, 41 players, 9 weeks, concentrated at h25/h30. No product I have worked on could have answered that question.
- **The ledger caught a dead feature by agreement, not by error.** Week 5: production rule frequencies matched the corpus (which runs `history: []`) within 1.5 points at every bucket. A live novelty governor should have pulled production *away* from the corpus. Three lines of grep, four minutes, and the governor had been dead since launch.
- **Arithmetic impossibility is the cheapest alarm there is.** `noteCount > attemptCount` in week 2 found the 18% practice-note over-count. `notes-per-recorded-attempt 0.98 → 0.86` in a day found the silent-ledger regression in Q4.

Honourable mentions, briefly: **refusing the LLM three times** (runtime paraphrase Q1, offline variant table Q2, pattern narrative Q3) held a line that would have made every later instrument meaningless — while *accepting* the model as an offline adversarial reader, which found the `green-rate` referent defect that three quarters of green CI structurally could not. And **stake-in-strokes plus `verify()`** is why we could delete seven rules in a year and defend each deletion with a number instead of a taste argument.

---

## 3. WHAT WE GOT WRONG

**The most expensive mistake: we pooled the transfer probe across holes for three quarters, and steered the roadmap with a number that was mostly hole memory.**

The probe's situation class is `(category, dominant penal feature kind, MOVE side)`. It never cared which hole the previous encounter was on. When Q3 finally split it: same-hole 64%, cross-hole 53%, over-18 cross-hole 50% — the null. Q4's held-out novel geometry came back 52% (n=486), over-18 49%. The headline had been 58% for a year and it had never measured course management.

**What it cost.** Two quarters of engineering were justified, in part, by a teaching number that was measuring recall:

- Q2 shipped the tier fix on the strength of `cheap-bail` "recovering" 61% → 66%. That recovery was substantially within-hole.
- Q2's single good result — over-18 transfer 54% → 58% — was, per Q3, *entirely* within-hole. We reported it hedged for the exclusion confound and missed the confound that mattered.
- Q3 built a pattern registry, a second golden corpus, a schema migration, three eligibility gates and a pocket card, and bought two hole traces. All defensible work; all planned under the belief that the note transferred at 58%.
- The spec's vision paragraph — "the fastest scoring gains available to a mid-handicap player" — stood a year longer than the evidence supported, and retracting it in Q4 was the largest single item in the quarter.

**The earliest available signal, and we had it in week 5 of Q1.** Two of them, in the same census:

1. The probe drew 612 qualifying pairs from a **26-puzzle library** in which 22 of 61 active players had already exhausted everything and entered `pickNext`'s review-by-recency mode. 33% of review re-serves reproduced the previous attempt's rule sequence; 14% were byte-identical. A "same-class re-encounter" in that library is overwhelmingly *the same hole, often the same puzzle*. We wrote both facts down in the same document and did not connect them.
2. `NoteEvent` → `Attempt` → `Puzzle` → `holeId`. The split cost nothing to compute. It was one `GROUP BY` away from the day the ledger shipped.

We also wrote, in Q1's own decision list, that we rejected `sgLoss` delta because "difficulty and skill move together and the delta measures neither." We were capable of that kind of reasoning about confounds. We just did not apply it to the metric we liked.

**Runners-up, so year two knows the shape of them.** `Puzzle.rating` as one scalar computed at one bucket: identified Q1 (150 Elo mean drift, Spearman 0.46 h15↔h30), deferred, cut as a design, untouched twice more. Four quarters, never once argued above the thing in front of it — the longest-standing known defect in the product. And shipping the note a quarter before its receipt, which left 1,880 attempts permanently opaque and a governor dead in production for five weeks.

---

## 4. WHAT WE LEARNED ABOUT TEACHING

Stated as claims. Disagree with them with data, not with intuition.

**C1. A place to aim changes an aim. A description of trouble does not.** The offset stake — a leader line from the player's pin to `correction.aim`, labelled with the yardage the sentence already prints — moved `movedToward` on skimmed notes from 54% to 59% (n=610/688, players active in both windows). The hazard callout, same design, same discipline, one quarter later: 57% → 58%. The difference is that one draws an instruction and one draws a diagnosis. *Counter-reading you should test:* both are pre/post, neither is randomized, and cache-miss and cohort composition are not fully controlled.

**C2. The sentence is a caption, and past the fortieth note it is barely a caption.** dwell by attempt index: 4.8s → 3.1s → 1.9s → 1.5s. 47% of notes past a player's 40th are under 1.2 seconds against a 24-word median note that needs 3.6s at a hard skim. Then Q4 deleted 19% of all sentences outright and *nothing detectable got worse* — notes per session 11.4 → 11.7, next-attempt sgLoss unchanged, post-silent re-aim 8.1% vs 7.6%. That is the strongest available evidence those sentences did nothing, and it is also exactly what you would see if none of them do.

**C3. A per-shot claim repeated is not a habit changed.** `clamp-habit` — "on 14 of your last 38 tee shots you aimed past your own driver carry" — fires on players who have read the `club-clamp` sentence dozens of times. Repetition of a true measurement is not instruction.

**C4. What the probe called teaching was mostly recall, and recall is real but not ours.** 65% same-hole against 52–53% cross- and novel-hole. A golfer who plays one course every week would be well served by recall. We have twelve holes, none of which is anyone's course, so counting recall would be measuring the product's ability to teach its own furniture.

**C5. Variety is not free and not neutral — it is paid for out of the note's most material claim unless something stops it.** Routing MOVE through the novelty governor demoted `cheap-bail` (67%) in favour of floor-stake maxims; on those 64 notes transfer was 52%, chance, and 41 of them went to over-18 handicaps. The tier rule (novelty reorders only within a stake tier) is the general form of the fix, and year two should assume any diversity mechanism has this failure mode until it proves otherwise.

**C6. Truth is necessary, insufficient, and narrower than we claimed.** `verify()` asserts `claim.text.includes(the computed figure)` — it proves the numeral, not its referent. `green-rate` printed "finds the green 34% of the time, averaging 25 feet from the flag," where `greenFeet` is computed over on-green samples only. Entailed; misread by any competent reader. Separately, on `sawgrass-17-approach-1` at h30 the note read "Even the best line here leaves 27% of a pattern in trouble. Yours left 30%" — every word entailed, nothing taught, because the puzzle had no decision in it. Truth is a floor, and the two things above it (comprehension, and whether the situation contains a lesson) are both unchecked by CI.

**C7. Curriculum outperformed copy.** The only intervention associated with an sgLoss movement in the over-18 group was *not serving them a puzzle* (0.16 → 0.13, confounded by construction, stated as such). Nothing we did to sentences ever moved sgLoss.

**C8. Player-model claims are gated by content, not by statistics.** `lateral-bias` is honest only with a both-side hazard gate, because the library puts its trouble left 28 times in 47 and the entire recovery class is 6/0 left. Tendency-card eligibility went 19/198 → 44/262 (17%) *while the library grew*, because the binding gate was never the one that loosens. The card failed Q3's own pre-registered 40% test and we halved it.

## 6. YEAR TWO'S THESIS

**The reveal is the teacher; the note is its caption; and the defensible product is a curriculum, not a copywriter.** One year of evidence says text interventions move nothing we can measure and one drawing intervention moved five points on exactly the population that does not read; that 19% of our sentences could be deleted with no detectable effect; that the only thing associated with a player losing fewer strokes was changing which puzzle they were served; and that the claim we cut from the spec — transfer to unseen holes — has fallen toward the null every single time we sharpened the measurement. So year two stops treating the rule registry as the product surface and treats it as a caption layer on a much richer drawing, spends its engineering on the queue that decides what a player sees, and either buys the evidence for a large claim or builds honestly to the small one. The small claim — *trains target selection on the holes it contains* — is not a failure; it is a specification, and nobody has yet built the product it actually describes.

**Three things this implies we must build.**

1. **Instruction geometry as the primary artefact.** Generalize the offset stake: the corridor, the dispersion overlay, the reachable-vs-clamped band, the bail-out target, all drawn at the resolution a 1.2-second look can consume, with the sentence demoted to a caption that may be absent. This is also the one place a clean experiment is available without withholding instruction from anyone — *drawing variant A vs drawing variant B*, both arms carrying a MOVE, which is the randomized test Q2 correctly refused to run by withholding.
2. **Per-bucket curriculum — the four-quarter zombie, finally.** Exclusion answered "is there a decision here"; nothing has ever answered "is this the right decision, next, for this player." `Puzzle.rating` is one scalar with 150 Elo of known drift and ρ=0.46 to a 28-handicap's real difficulty ordering. The blocker was always the same: the ordering would inherit an extrapolated sigma. Year two resolves that directly — freeze the constants and own the extrapolation in writing, or buy a launch monitor and stop extrapolating — and then ships the ordering. This is also the only credible answer to the 500-note player, who currently gets 62 of their last 100 notes on a puzzle they have played three or more times, and whom Q4 served by getting quieter.
3. **The field, precomputed offline at a finer resolution.** `GRID_SPACING_YDS 6` and `MC_SAMPLES 600` are set by the 900ms reveal, and they are why `merion-11` — correctly traced — is unsayable, why `floors.anchor` is 0.08, why the fallback path exists at all, and why a constants change is a 19-minute operational event with the reveal at risk. Precompute the grids offline at real resolution, delete the worker fallback, and content authoring becomes the whole cost — which is the honest shape of this product anyway, and which is what makes (1) affordable.

**The one thing we must finally kill: the explanation rule registry as a place where product work happens.** Freeze it at nine. No new READ or MOVE rules in year two; `rulesVersion` bumps only for deletions and truth fixes. The cost census is unambiguous — defect count does not track firing share, `fallback-strokes` fires on 3.1% of notes and caused two of the year's five field escapes, and every rule we added or tuned moved no measurable outcome while every rule we deleted cost nothing detectable. The READ side is four rules wide and one is a backstop; `depth`, `lie`, `green`, `room` and `leave` are all empty tags. Stop defending that as a rule system. It is a template with a switch, it is fine as a template with a switch, and the attention it consumes belongs to the drawing and the queue.

*(Two live consequences to accept rather than relitigate: layups — the one category we paid for a trace to get — now have `aim-line` and nothing else, and `club-clamp` still prints two placeholder constants times a self-reported club speed to the person best placed to know it is wrong, on 44% of h5 READs, because the `HeatmapCache` key cannot carry a per-club table. Under a frozen registry, the second one is fixed by (3), not by a new rule.)*

## 7. THE STANDING RISK

**The risk: every instrument in this system fails toward "everything is fine."** This is not a hypothetical; it happened five times in four quarters, and it is structural rather than accidental.

- The novelty governor was dead for five weeks and produced *perfect agreement* with the corpus. Health and death look identical.
- A `verify()` failure and a guard returning false were the same row in the census until Q2 built the drop log — so a claim that silently stops shipping just makes the note quieter, and 1.1% of production notes were doing that.
- The first drift census reported `sub-resolution` and `aim-line` as *insensitive* to `LATERAL_SIGMA_BY_HANDICAP` — because the corpus read `sigmaLat` out of a committed fixture. A gate reporting "no drift" is indistinguishable from a gate that is broken.
- The silence rule made the ledger stop writing rows for three weeks, in the quarter whose central intervention was silence. Orphan rate stayed flat at 1.4%, because orphan rate cannot see a row that was never written.
- Twice, an instrument arrived carrying the assumption it was built to test, and both times the wrong version was the flattering one (dwell over-counted, drift under-reported). We have no process fix for that. We have a habit of checking the flattering result first.

**The specific way this kills the feature quietly in year two:** someone tunes a constant. It is a one-line commit, CI stays green, every sentence remains perfectly entailed — by different numbers. Per the drift census, a ±20% move in `LATERAL_SIGMA_BY_HANDICAP` flips 7.8% of notes between *"that is the line, play it again"* and *"aim 14 yards left"* — the do-something/do-nothing instruction, aimed at the widest-dispersion players, turning on an extrapolated placeholder. Simultaneously every `HeatmapCache` row invalidates (~19 minutes of cold start, fallback share to ~30%, the 900ms reveal genuinely at risk), and the ledger splits into two halves that `constantsHash` will correctly refuse to pool — so the metrics go quiet at exactly the moment the product changes. Nothing alarms. The graphs just get thinner.

**The tripwire: assert a non-zero floor on every instrument, not just a ceiling.** Every gate we own checks that a bad number stays below a bound. None of them check that the instrument is still alive. Concretely, and all of these are cheap:

1. **Ratio liveness, continuous.** `notes-per-recorded-attempt` must sit in [0.95, 1.02] — this caught the silent ledger in a day. Add: dwell samples per printed note ≈ 1; drop-log rate strictly in (0, 1.5%) — *zero is an alarm, not a pass*; `silent: true` share within a band of its expected value.
2. **Anti-agreement alarm.** Production rule frequencies vs. the golden corpus at the same `rulesVersion` and `constantsHash`, weekly. Alarm on divergence >3 points **and on agreement tighter than 0.5 points**, because a novelty governor that is working must pull production away from a `history: []` corpus. Exact agreement is the signature of a dead mechanism.
3. **Drift-gate self-test.** `explain:drift` must assert its own *known-sensitive* rows: if a run reports `sub-resolution`/`aim-line` as insensitive to `LATERAL_SIGMA_BY_HANDICAP`, or `hold-the-line`'s corridor as insensitive to `GRID_SPACING_YDS`, the gate has failed — fail the job, do not report green. A gate must be able to detect the thing it exists to detect, on demand, every run.
4. **A constants-change runbook, gated.** Touching `lib/engine/constants.ts` requires: a fresh `explain:drift` diff in review, a stated cache cold-start window, a `constantsHash` bump, and an explicit acknowledgment that the ledger is now two unpoolable halves. Until that runbook exists, the honest position is that **engine constants are frozen for v1** — and if they are frozen, `LATERAL_SIGMA_BY_HANDICAP`'s extrapolation past handicap 20 is permanent and should be written into the spec as a property of the product, not left as a placeholder comment inviting someone to "tune freely."

And one non-mechanical tripwire, because the two defects a machine could never have caught this year were both caught by a person reading one rendered artefact: **keep the corpus reading rota, keep it at 40 sampled notes minimum, and keep the LLM critic advisory.** A human reading real output found "this is `room` again" in a single pass, on a sentence that three CI gates and a `verify()` had all passed. That signature in `corpus-read.json` is the cheapest thing in the build and the only check we have on what the product actually *means*.
